## 1. Who are the meaningful local and regional players, and what does each sell?

### Takeaway
Nigerian high-stakes hiring tests are dominated by two incumbents, Dragnet Solutions (CBT plus physical test centres, and the exclusive sub-Saharan distribution partner for US simulation vendor HR Avatar) and Workforce Group (bespoke cognitive and psychometric tests, banks-heavy by reputation), with Jobberman selling iMocha-powered soft-skill assessments as a flat-fee add-on to job listings. Nobody found in this research sells a Nigeria-built, job-specific behavioural simulation for contact-centre or agent-banking roles; the closest threats are Dragnet's HR Avatar licence in Nigeria and ShiftMate's AI call-centre simulations in South Africa.

### Inferences
- Dragnet is the incumbent to plan around. It combines the three things a newcomer lacks (test centres, procurement relationships with oil and gas, FMCG, telco and banks, and ISO certifications) and already holds regional rights to a simulation library. The open question is whether it actually sells HR Avatar simulations at volume for frontline roles or keeps them as a catalogue item behind its core graduate-CBT business; nothing found shows Nigerian contact-centre deployments.
- Workforce Group and Proten are both assessment vendors and outsourcing/staffing firms, so each is simultaneously a competitor and a possible channel.
- Jobberman's assessment is a generic, imported (iMocha) library priced per listing. That pricing suits low-volume professional hiring and gives the buyer unlimited candidates for one fee, which undercuts any per-candidate price on high-volume roles unless the newcomer wins on relevance and fraud resistance.
- South Africa is the only market where a contact-centre-specific simulation vendor (ShiftMate) was found, and where regulation (HPCSA classification, Employment Equity Act validation duty) shapes vendor claims. Nigeria has no equivalent rule surfaced in this research, which lowers entry barriers but also removes a compliance-driven reason to buy.
- No player was found positioned on Nigerian frontline behavioural simulation built for local roles. Absence of evidence here is weak, given the blocked fetches.

## 2. What do large Nigerian employers actually use today to screen frontline and graduate hires, and who runs their aptitude tests?

### Takeaway
For graduate intakes the pattern is an outsourced online aptitude test followed by a supervised re-test, run mainly by Dragnet or Workforce Group, with multinationals and the Big Four using SHL, TalentQ or in-house tests. For frontline roles at fintechs the evidence points to in-house screening on ordinary ATSs with typing thresholds and interviews, and no named assessment vendor.

- PalmPay: a 2022 customer-service posting required 45 wpm or more, measured on a one-minute English passage on a public typing-test site [job posting, 2022] — [HotNigerianJobs](https://www.hotnigerianjobs.com/print/470994/). ### Inferences
- Graduate testing is a consolidated, vendor-run market; frontline testing is fragmented, in-house and low-tech. The second is the open space, but it also means there is no existing budget line for frontline assessment to capture.
- Fintechs screen frontline support on prior experience and typing speed, which are cheap proxies. A simulation has to beat "free typing site plus interview" on cost per good hire, not beat Dragnet.
- Because the bank-to-vendor mapping comes only from prep sites that contradict each other, the report should not state which vendor serves which bank as fact.

### Gaps
- No employer confirmed its vendor on the record except by implication (KPMG's own test; Dragnet's Chevron statement).
- Nothing found on how MTN, Airtel, Access, GTBank or Zenith screen contact-centre agents specifically, or whether their outsourced partners do it for them.
- OPay and PalmPay vendor arrangements, if any, are unknown.

## 3. How do Nigerian contact centres and BPOs currently screen agents, and who supplies the tools?

### Takeaway
Candidate accounts describe a short, manual sequence: written or aptitude test, typing test for speed and accuracy, basic computer-proficiency check, and an oral interview, sometimes within two days. No third-party assessment supplier was named for any Nigerian BPO, and no evidence of Versant-type voice tests or simulations in Nigerian centres was found.

### Inferences
- Nigerian BPO screening tools appear to be built or improvised in-house (free typing sites, paper or simple CBT tests, interviewer judgement). That means low switching cost but also low willingness to pay per candidate.

### Gaps

## 4. What are the known pain points with current local screening?

### Takeaway
Three problems are well evidenced: an open trade in "past questions" for named vendors' tests, impersonation and leakage in computer-based recruitment exams, and a large fraud economy in which fake aptitude tests and fees are used to extract money from jobseekers. Evidence on bias and on irrelevance to the job exists mainly as opinion and one academic chapter, not as measured data.

### Cited Findings
- Recycled items and prep industry: vendor-specific past-question packs for Dragnet, Workforce and KPMG are sold and given away; an "All-in-One Nigerian Aptitude Test & Past Questions Pack" is sold on Selar [prep-site] — [GSE Solutions blog](https://gsesolutions.blogspot.com/), [JobsRegion](https://www.jobsregion.com/dragnet-past-questions/), [Selar](https://selar.com/7538398c6j). Government-exam past questions sell for ₦1,000-₦5,000 on social media, with no official versions; a seller was arrested during DSS recruitment [press, September 2025] — [Nigeria Startup Act news](https://www.nigeriastartupact.ng/ignore-ncs-past-questions-sold-online-focus-on-pre-test-cbt/)
- Cheating in aptitude tests: a JarusHub piece argues the tests advance "the lucky, the informed, the strong-willed and cheats"; its 2018 version alleges some candidates see likely questions in advance or offer "something" to be moved to the next stage [opinion/anecdote; neither names a vendor] — [JarusHub](https://jarushub.com/how-nigerian-candidates-now-cheat-in-aptitude-tests), [2018 repost](https://updatemedailyblog.blogspot.com/2018/03/how-nigerian-job-seekers-now-cheat-in.html)
- Unsupervised online stage: prep sites note calculators are freely usable on at-home tests but banned at assessment centres, and vendors re-test in person because of it [prep-site; self-claim] — [TestStreams blog](https://teststreams.com/blog/?p=48659), [Dragnet blog](https://dragnetnigeria-h8e8aeeab5dkg0cg.northcentralus-01.azurewebsites.net/?p=378)
- Impersonation: a 33-year-old man was arrested sitting an FCTA promotion exam for his father [press, October 2026] — [Sahara Reporters](https://saharareporters.com/2026/10/07/33-year-old-man-arrested-taking-fcta-promotion-exam-behalf-his-father). An NAQS recruitment exercise drew applicant allegations of fraud [press, August 2026; title only] — [Sahara Reporters](https://saharareporters.com/2026/08/26/nigerian-agency-naqs-recruitment-scandal-applicants-cry-foul-over-alleged-fraud-secret). An older report said CBT "did not check impersonation" and that most centres had the questions the night before [press, about 2011; URL attribution uncertain] — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2011/05/corruption-rocks-customs-recruitment-exercise/)
- Technology-assisted cheating in exams (AI tools, UV pens) is documented for school exams, and WAEC and NECO are due to move fully to CBT by 2027 [press] — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/?p=162296)
- Test-centre reliability: Dragnet had to rebut reports of a system collapse at Chevron's 26 October 2024 test [press] — [Niger Delta Today](https://nigerdeltatoday.com/?p=32151)
- Scams: fraudsters stage fake aptitude tests and interviews to make jobs look real [opinion] — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/opinion/article/the-rise-of-recruitment-fraud-in-nigeria-how-job-seekers-can-stay-safe/); one pattern has everyone "pass" and then charges US$90-150 for a separate online psychometric test [job-board account] — [NGCareers](https://ngcareers.com/fake-companies); a candidate was asked for about ₦20,000 after a test and interview at a fake company [first-person account] — [Zikoko](https://www.zikoko.com/money/5-nigerians-talk-about-their-fake-job-interview-experiences/); paid "slot" schemes are reported [press] — [HumAngle](https://www.humanglemedia.com/the-exploitative-slot-systems-targeting-desperate-job-seekers-in-nigeria); a "₦40 billion job scam crisis" prompted tougher vetting of job ads [press, April 2025] — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/story/naira40-billion-job-scam-crisis-triggers-tougher-vetting-for-employment-ads-2025042315454969951)
- Official warnings: EFCC, Dangote Cement, the Federal Ministry of Finance (July 2026) and Customs (fake aptitude-test notice, July 2025) have all warned against payment demands and fake test notices — [EFCC](https://www.efcc.gov.ng/efcc/news-and-information/news-release/2878-efcc-raises-alarm-over-job-scam), [Global Cement](https://globalcement.com/news/12704-dangote-cement-warns-public-against-recruitment-scam), [Premium Times](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/899345-finance-ministry-warns-job-seekers-against-extortion.html), [Nigeria Maritime 360](https://nigeriamaritime360.com/customs-warns-public-of-fake-aptitude-test)
- Job boards carry a standing banner on listings: "Never pay for any CBT, test or assessment as part of any recruitment process" — [MyJobMag](https://www.myjobmag.com/jobs/ux-writer-at-moniepoint-inc-formerly-teamapt-inc)
- Bias and fairness: a Cambridge University Press volume includes a chapter "Twenty questions about employment testing bias and unfairness in Nigeria" (existence confirmed, contents not read) [research] — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/global-perspectives-on-the-definition-assessment-and-reduction-of-bias-and-unfairness-in-employment-testing/twenty-questions-about-employment-testing-bias-and-unfairness-in-nigeria/0FEC0B69C264E5AAAEA5CCF3F9A64A4E)

### Inferences
- A product whose content cannot be memorised (branching simulations, generated scenarios) addresses the best-evidenced weakness of incumbent tests, which is item exposure through past-question trading.
- Any new assessment brand will be imitated by scammers. Candidate-facing trust design (never charging candidates, verifiable invitations from the employer's domain) is a launch requirement, not a nicety.
- The two-stage "online then supervised re-test" model is itself evidence that vendors do not trust remote results. A mobile-only frontline product must answer the impersonation question directly, since employers have been trained to expect a proctored confirmation.

### Gaps
- No quantified prevalence of cheating on Dragnet or Workforce tests, and no documented vendor-side leak.
- Nothing specific on candidates' travel and data costs for test centres, or on test-centre geography outside Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt.
- No measured adverse-impact or validity data for any Nigerian vendor's tests.

## 5. Does any local player already offer job simulations or behavioural assessments on mobile, in Pidgin or local languages, or offline? Is anyone positioned on agent-banking agent vetting?

### Takeaway
No. The only simulation capability found in Nigeria is imported (HR Avatar through Dragnet), nothing found is delivered in Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo, and no vendor is positioned on agent-banking vetting, even though the Central Bank's October 2025 rules put due diligence and pre-onboarding training duties on principals and super-agents.

- Local-language speech models now exist (N-ATLAS), which removes a technical barrier to Pidgin or Hausa voice simulations that did not exist for earlier entrants.

### Gaps
- The full due-diligence checklist in the CBN guidelines was not read.

- OPay's and PalmPay's agent recruitment and vetting processes were not found.
