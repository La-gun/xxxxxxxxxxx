## 1. AI-enabled cheating: evidence 2024–2026, which test types are vulnerable, and how vendors and employers respond

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed work from 2024–2026 shows frontier LLMs moving from weak to near-ceiling on unproctored cognitive items within about a year (GPT-4 below the 20th percentile on a number-series test in 2024; o1 at about the 95th percentile in 2025), and self-reported candidate GenAI use in real pre-hire assessments rising from 2024 to 2025 (over 23% admitting some use in an unwarned control group in Q3 2025). Evidence is strongest for text-based ability and knowledge tests, mixed for personality and SJTs, and I found no independent published study on whether game-based assessments or interactive simulations resist AI assistance — that claim currently rests on vendor assertion.

### Cited Findings

**Published studies on LLM capability against selection tests**
- [PEER] Hickman, Dunlop & Wolf (2024, International Journal of Selection and Assessment) tested GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 on a quantitative ability test (number-series completion) and a verbal ability test (judge whether a statement is true from a passage). The LLMs performed substantially better on the verbal test than on the quantitative one — [ResearchGate record of the IJSA paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380672896_The_performance_of_large_language_models_on_quantitative_and_verbal_ability_tests_Initial_evidence_and_implications_for_unproctored_high-stakes_testing)
- [PEER] In the 2025 follow-up, Hickman reports that GPT-4 had scored below the 20th percentile on that quantitative test, whereas o1-mini answered 18 of 20 items correctly (88th percentile) and the full o1 model scored at about the 95th percentile, with even higher scores on verbal ability. Figures are from the preprint text as surfaced by search; not checked against the journal's final version — [Hickman 2025, "All Your Base Are Belong to Us", IJSA 33(2), e70005 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389428748_All_your_base_are_belong_to_us_The_urgent_reality_of_unproctored_testing_in_the_age_of_LLMs); [Wiley record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijsa.70005?af=R)
- [PEER] The same 2025 paper cites other work to say LLMs "can perform well" on personality tests (Phillips & Robie 2024), situational judgment tests (Mittelstädt et al. 2024), interviews (Canagasuriam & Lukacik 2025) and verbal ability tests. I did not retrieve those three papers' own effect sizes — [Hickman 2025 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389428748_All_your_base_are_belong_to_us_The_urgent_reality_of_unproctored_testing_in_the_age_of_LLMs)
- [SECONDARY, university press office, May 2025] Hickman's stated conclusion: few, if any, unproctored tests will remain safe from people using large language models to cheat; the piece is headlined "The days of unproctored testing may be over" — [Virginia Tech News, May 2025](https://news.vt.edu/articles/2025/05/science-unproctored-testing-hickman.html)
- [PEER] Personality faking is less clear-cut. Robie et al. (2025, IJSA) found that ChatGPT showed some ability to manipulate a personality assessment, but without coaching it did not consistently outperform humans at faking while avoiding detection; coaching had minimal effect on impression-management scores for humans or ChatGPT, but reduced overclaiming (OCQ) bias scores for ChatGPT — [Robie et al. 2025, IJSA](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijsa.70015)
- [PEER] Lievens & Dunlop (IJSA 33(1), e12516; first online Dec 2024) argue for "a more nuanced view": they set out three scenarios for how applicant GenAI use could affect mean scores and validity, including one in which validity improves because GenAI acts as an "equalizer" (familiarisation with formats, rehearsal of interview answers). A review citing this work describes the research evidence on whether generative AI can solve SJTs as mixed — [Lievens & Dunlop, IJSA](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ijsa.12516); [SMU repository record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/7700/)
- [PEER] Mirowska (2025, IJSA commentary) agrees GenAI use should not be "blindly feared" but warns of longer-term harm to job-performance prediction and workplace relationships if use goes unexamined — [Mirowska 2025, IJSA](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijsa.70017)

**Published evidence on how often candidates actually use GenAI**
- [PEER] Robie, Wingate, Baytalskaya & Butera (2026, IJSA 34(2), e70056): self-reported GenAI use on pre-hire assessments was higher in 2025 than in 2024. In Study 2 (N = 3,356, Q3 2025, an assessment with cognitive and non-cognitive components), more than 23% of control participants and 10.6% of participants shown a consequences-based warning admitted at least some GenAI use. Most users applied it to problem-solving items or to clarify instructions, and few saw it as highly effective at raising their score — [Robie et al. 2026, IJSA](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijsa.70056); [SSRN preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6381418)
- [SECONDARY, vendor blog relaying the 2024 wave] A 2024 sample of 5,675 applicants: fewer than 3% said they used generative AI during a pre-hire assessment, rising to 19% when search engines were included. Whether this is Study 1 of the paper above was not confirmed — [AssessAll blog](https://www.assessall.com/blog/fewer-than-3-of-applicants-admitted-using-ai-your-problem-is-variance-not)

**Industry and vendor numbers (not comparable to each other; mostly from parties that sell detection)**
- [VENDOR] Fabric analysed nearly 20,000 AI-run interviews (July 2025–January 2026) and flagged 38.5% of candidates as showing signs of cheating, highest in technical roles. This is one platform's own detection flags, not confirmed cheating — [The Interview Guys summary](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=17305); [Fabric](https://fabrichq.ai/blogs/state-of-cheating-in-interviews-in-2026-tools-trends-and-prevention)
- [VENDOR survey] Resume Genius 2026 survey of 1,000 US hiring managers: 28% said candidates had used AI to cheat on skills assessments — [Resume Genius](https://resumegenius.com/blog/ai-impact-on-hiring-2026)
- [VENDOR survey via trade press, May 2026] Greenhouse 2026 report: recruiters' most common observations in the past year were fake references (51%), CV exaggeration (35%) and candidates using AI during interviews (32%). Another vendor blog quotes the Greenhouse AI-in-interview figure as 35%, so the exact number is unsettled — [FM Magazine, May 2026](https://www.fm-magazine.com/news/2026/may/ai-hiring-saves-time-but-fraud-risks-are-growing/); conflicting figure in [TheHireHub](https://www.thehirehub.ai/blog/ai-interview-cheating-detection)
- [SECONDARY] A Fabric-based figure relayed by SHRM Labs: 59% of hiring managers suspect candidates of using AI tools during live assessments (suspicion, not detection) — [SHRM Labs](https://shrm.org/labs/resources/when-candidates-can-fake-skills-real-time-assessment-design)
- [SECONDARY, primary Gartner release not opened] Gartner: a 2Q25 survey of 3,000 job candidates found 6% admitted to interview fraud; Gartner forecasts that roughly one in four candidate profiles worldwide will be fake by 2028 (a forecast, not a measured rate); 39% of US hiring managers say they are conducting more in-person interviews to verify authenticity — relayed in [Proctor360 blog](https://proctor360.com/blog/applicants-cheat-pre-hire-assessments) and [Forbes, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/karadennison/2026/08/17/the-rise-of-ai-cheating-culture-and-the-hiring-crisis-it-left-behind/)
- [SECONDARY] A counter-view exists in trade press: a UK HR outlet ran "Fears of AI cheating in hiring 'overblown' as employers urged to rethink assessments" (headline only retrieved) — [HRreview](https://hrreview.co.uk/hr-news/recruitment/fears-of-ai-cheating-in-hiring-overblown/387511?amp=1)

**How employers and vendors are responding**
- [PEER] A 2026 IJSA paper by Dunlop and colleagues, "From Forbid to Reimagine: Employer Strategies for Responding to Candidate GenAI Use in Assessment", exists and frames the response space as a spectrum from prohibition to redesign. Only the title was retrieved — [Dunlop et al. 2026, IJSA](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijsa.70060)
- [PEER] Warnings work partially: a consequences-based warning cut admitted use from over 23% to 10.6% in Q3 2025 — [Robie et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijsa.70056)
- [SECONDARY] Gartner's recommended sequence, as relayed: state acceptable AI use up front, tell candidates fraud detection is in place, include in-person steps, then add system-level validation such as tighter background checks and identity verification — [Proctor360 blog](https://proctor360.com/blog/applicants-cheat-pre-hire-assessments)
- [SECONDARY] Return to in-person is described as real but mostly anecdotal: for example the firm Nominal requires final interviews in person, with engineers taking coding tests on company laptops — [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/ai-job-interview-cheating-debate). A Wharton opinion piece argues the best prevention is to assess in person — [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/opinion/cheating-in-hiring-its-a-messy-expensive-ai-world/). A September 2026 piece reports employers adding camera pans and "hand wave" checks to remote interviews — [Allwork.Space, Sept 2026](https://allwork.space/2026/09/employers-are-changing-job-interviews-to-catch-ai-cheating-from-camera-pans-to-hand-wave-tests/)
- [SECONDARY] SHRM Labs' position: assessments that are adaptive, scenario-based and linked to actual job tasks are "extremely difficult" for AI tools to cheat on. This is an assertion in an association article, not a study — [SHRM Labs](https://shrm.org/labs/resources/when-candidates-can-fake-skills-real-time-assessment-design)
- [VENDOR] Proctoring vendors describe AI proctoring of video, audio and browser activity plus identity verification as the standard counter-measure — [Glider AI](https://glider.ai/candidate-cheating-interviews-assessments-guide/); [Hyring](https://hyring.com/blog/7-ways-candidates-cheat-using-ai-and-how-proctoring-stops-it/)
- [SECONDARY, vendor blog; original study not opened] AI-text detectors are reported to falsely flag non-native English writers 61% of the time — [Matchcard blog](https://matchcard.co.uk/blog/ai-cheating-in-hiring-assessments/)

### Inferences
- Vulnerability ranking supported by the evidence found: (most exposed) unproctored verbal reasoning and knowledge tests, then numerical/abstract reasoning (exposed since reasoning models, 2025), then text-based SJTs and written or asynchronous interview answers (LLMs perform well, evidence mixed on SJTs), then personality (LLMs can fake, but did not beat motivated humans without coaching). Game-based assessments and interactive simulations sit in an "unknown" category: no independent study either way was found.
- The speed of change is itself a finding: a format judged safe in a 2024 paper (number series, GPT-4 below 20th percentile) was broken by 2025. Any "AI-resistant" claim should be treated as having a short shelf life and needing recurring red-team evidence.
- Actual candidate use is far lower than model capability implies, and far lower than detection vendors' headline figures (peer-reviewed self-report: under 3% in 2024 for GenAI, over 23% unwarned in Q3 2025; vendor flags: 38.5%). Self-report under-counts and vendor flags over-count; the true rate for a given frontline role is unknown.
- For a Nigeria-first venture, text-detection-based integrity controls carry a specific fairness risk if the 61% false-flag figure for non-native English writers holds; behavioural, timed, multi-step task evidence and identity assurance look like safer foundations than "AI-written text" detection.
- Supervised or in-person rounds are regaining ground as the trusted check. A design that pairs a cheap remote behavioural screen with a short verified re-test is consistent with where employers are heading.

### Gaps
- No independent published study found on LLM or agent performance against game-based assessments or interactive job simulations. Vendor claims of resistance (for example from task-based assessment providers) were not retrieved and would be marketing in any case.
- Effect sizes from Mittelstädt et al. 2024 (SJT), Phillips & Robie 2024 (personality) and Canagasuriam & Lukacik 2025 (interviews) were not retrieved; only their citation in Hickman 2025.
- Content of Dunlop et al. 2026 ("From Forbid to Reimagine") not retrieved.
- Specific product responses by SHL, HireVue, TestGorilla and others (proctoring tiers, "AI-allowed" assessments, verified re-tests) were not retrieved; the search budget ended before those queries ran. Reports that large technology employers reinstated in-person interview rounds in 2025 were not confirmed in this session.
- No data found on GenAI use rates among African or Nigerian candidates specifically.
- The Robie 2026 paper's breakdown of use by assessment type, if any, was not retrieved.

## 2. Validity and fairness science: work samples, SJTs, simulations, game-based assessment, and cross-cultural validity in Africa

### Takeaway
The 2022 Sackett et al. revision lowered headline validities by roughly .10–.20 and put structured interviews (.42), job knowledge tests (.40), biodata (.38) and work samples (.33) ahead of cognitive ability (.31), which helps a simulation-led product; but work samples and SJTs carry larger subgroup differences in applicant samples than older figures suggested (Black–White d of about .67–.73 for work samples, .66 for SJTs in one large applicant sample), driven by cognitive loading. Game-based assessment still lacks a criterion-validity meta-analysis, and I found no published Nigerian validation study of a selection test, only South African bias and invariance work.

### Cited Findings

**Meta-analytic validity**
- [PEER] Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens (2022), "Revisiting meta-analytic estimates of validity in personnel selection: Addressing systematic overcorrection for restriction of range", Journal of Applied Psychology 107(11), 2040–2068, argued that prior meta-analyses (the basis of Schmidt & Hunter 1998) applied range-restriction corrections that inflated validity. The same procedures stay near the top in rank, but mean validity estimates fall by about .10–.20 — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357440267_Revisiting_meta-analytic_estimates_of_validity_in_personnel_selection_Addressing_systematic_overcorrection_for_restriction_of_range); [2023 focal article, Industrial and Organizational Psychology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/industrial-and-organizational-psychology/article/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D)
- [SECONDARY summaries of the 2022 table; consistent across several sources] Revised mean operational validities: structured interviews .42, job knowledge tests .40, empirically keyed biodata .38, work sample tests .33, cognitive ability .31, unstructured interviews .19 — [Olive summary](https://olive.is/answers/hiring-methods-predict-performance/); [Noon summary](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/356-structured-interview-validity); [SIOP TIP article on whether cognitive ability is the best predictor](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance)
- [SECONDARY summary of the same table] Mean US Black–White subgroup differences: structured interviews d = .23, work samples d = .67, cognitive ability d = .79 — [GSH summary of the 2022 table](https://gshfinancial.com/insights/bf-hiring-validity-sackett-2022-revision.html)
- [SECONDARY] Variability matters as much as the mean: the .42 for structured interviews carries an 80% credibility interval of roughly .18 to .66, so individual designs vary widely — [Noon summary](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/356-structured-interview-validity)
- [PEER] The revision is contested: a published commentary argues the revisions over-correct in the other direction and that range-restriction corrections remain appropriate for the vast majority of concurrent validity estimates (the search summary attributed this to Carretta & Ree; attribution not verified) — [Commentary in Industrial and Organizational Psychology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/5C25EFD82F603039E111FFAF299B1B0F/core-reader)
- [PEER] Berry, Lievens, Zhang & Sackett's updated personnel-selection meta-analytic matrix concludes the validity–diversity trade-off conversation should shift from the role of cognitive ability tests to that of other selection methods — [University of Minnesota record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/insights-from-an-updated-personnel-selection-meta-analytic-matrix/)
- [PEER] Roth, Bobko & McFarland's meta-analysis of work sample validity ("Updating and integrating some classic literature", Personnel Psychology 2005) found an observed mean correlation of .26 between work sample tests and job performance measures — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227526262_A_meta-analysis_of_work_sample_test_validity_Updating_and_integrating_some_classic_literature)

**Subgroup differences**
- [PEER] Roth, Bobko, McFarland & Buster (2008, Personnel Psychology 61), "Work sample tests in personnel selection: a meta-analysis of Black–White differences in overall and exercise scores": the older benchmark of d = .38 was affected by range restriction and inclusion of non-work-sample tests; in job-applicant samples the overall difference was d = .73. Exercises measuring cognitive and job-knowledge skills showed the largest gaps — [Library record of the paper](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_220143196)
- [PEER] Bobko & Roth (2013, Personnel Psychology) conclude that some predictors, work samples among them, can show differences similar in size to paper-and-pencil cognitive tests — [Zendy record, doi 10.1111/peps.12007](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/peps.12007)
- [PEER] Whetzel, McDaniel & Nguyen (2008), "Subgroup differences in situational judgment test performance: a meta-analysis": the cognitive saturation of an SJT is positively related to the size of ethnic subgroup differences (reported r = .77 between cognitive saturation and the Black–White gap) — [Scilit record](https://www.scilit.net/publications/40227427556efd603677a8dade8a8101)
- [PEER] Herde, Lievens & Shalfrooshan (2020): in an applicant sample of N = 37,530, Black–White SJT differences were d = 0.66, larger than the d = 0.38 typical of incumbent samples on which earlier estimates were based — [SMU repository record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6435)
- [PEER] A separate review states that mean race differences in SJT performance are largely explained by the SJT's cognitive loading — [PMC8451885](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8451885)

**Game-based assessment (GBA): limits of the evidence**
- [PEER] What exists is mainly construct evidence: a meta-analysis of the convergent validity of GBA against traditional measures (ResearchGate record dated 2025) and a 2024 meta-analysis of the relationship between game-related assessment and traditional cognitive ability measures — [Convergent Validity of Game-Based Assessment: A Meta-Analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397084854_Convergent_Validity_of_Game-Based_Assessment_A_Meta-Analysis); [Game-related assessment and traditional cognitive measures: a meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387112059_The_Relationship_Between_Game-Related_Assessment_and_Traditional_Measures_of_Cognitive_Ability-A_Meta-Analysis)
- [PEER] A study of cognitive GBAs in recruitment reported convergent validity of r = 0.5 with traditional tests and test–retest reliability of r = 0.68, and stated that studies of the psychometric properties and predictive validity of GBAs "are still lacking" — [PMC9891208](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9891208/)
- [PEER] A 2024 study of a serious game as an alternative to personality questionnaires noted in its literature review that there were no studies linking gamified assessments to job performance, and called for evidence beyond academic performance — [PMC11065274](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11065274/)
- [PEER] The best-known positive criterion study (gamified SJT of soft skills, Spanish Journal of Psychology) reported criterion and incremental validity over cognitive ability and personality, but the performance criterion was self-reported — [Spanish Journal of Psychology](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/spanish-journal-of-psychology/article/exploring-the-relationship-of-a-gamified-assessment-with-performance/39125B6F3BD27F6B4C1AD514D2330E4A)
- [VENDOR, UNVERIFIED] A vendor blog cites an observed r of about .30 (about .45 corrected) for a GBA from a 2023 paper; not checked against the original — [Talero blog](https://www.taleroai.com/blog/game-based-science)

**Cross-cultural validity and adaptation in African contexts**
- [PEER] South Africa, WAIS-III adaptation: verbal comprehension items were biased against test-takers who spoke an African language at home, even among those exposed to English daily — [Critically examining language bias in the South African adaptation of the WAIS-III](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47739563_Critically_examining_language_bias_in_the_South_African_adaptation_of_the_WAIS-III)
- [PEER] South African Personality Inventory, English version, across the four ethnocultural groups: configural and metric invariance held, scalar invariance did not — which limits fair comparison of raw scores across groups on a common norm — [Investigating measurement invariance in the SAPI: English version](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336206276_Investigating_measurement_invariance_in_the_South_African_Personality_Inventory_English_version)
- [PEER] An SA Journal of Industrial Psychology study of cognitive selection measures in the police service reported low construct bias and rare item-level bias (snippet-level; details not read) — [SAJIP article](https://sajip.co.za/index.php/sajip/article/download/182/178)
- [PEER] A South African workplace sample on the Montreal Cognitive Assessment confirmed invariance across gender and language, but the authors noted the sample had good English proficiency and called for replication in more linguistically diverse samples — [PMC12082251](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12082251/)
- [PEER] Nigeria: the searches found workplace scale validations but no criterion validation of a selection test. Examples: a nine-item work-assessment tool for 406 pharmaceutical sales executives with configural, metric, scalar and residual invariance across gender, training and experience — [Journal of Pharmaceutical Health Services Research 13(4)](https://academic.oup.com/jphsr/article/13/4/262/6761048); the Career Adapt-Abilities Scale–Nigeria form on 271 bank and hotel employees, with some loadings higher than the international sample — [Journal of Vocational Behavior](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001879116300574); a validation of psychological safety, empowerment and performance measures motivated by the point that most constructs were developed in Western cultures — [RAUSP Management Journal 57(3)](https://www.emerald.com/rausp/article/57/3/219/362312/Validation-of-the-psychological-safety)
- [SECONDARY, World Bank blog on the ESTEEM study] Across Côte d'Ivoire, Nigeria and Tanzania, behavioural assessments of socio-emotional skills predicted employment and income better than self-reports — [World Bank Development Talk blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/what-skills-really-count--examining-socioemotional-skills-that-m)

### Inferences
- The post-2022 science favours a product built on job-relevant work samples, job knowledge and structured behavioural evidence over a pure cognitive screen: comparable or better mean validity, and face validity that buyers and candidates understand.
- "Simulations are fairer" cannot be claimed by default. Work samples and SJTs show applicant-sample gaps of d about .66–.73 in US data, and the driver is cognitive and knowledge loading. A venture can design loading down (less reading, less abstract reasoning, more interpersonal and procedural content) and must then measure subgroup differences locally by language, schooling and region, since US race categories do not map to Nigeria.
- The ESTEEM result (behavioural measures beat self-report in Nigeria, Côte d'Ivoire and Tanzania) is directly supportive of behavioural or simulation measurement over self-report questionnaires in this market, and is one of the few data points that includes Nigeria.
- A published Nigerian criterion-validation study with local norms appears to be an open space. Scalar non-invariance in South African personality data shows why imported norms are a real weakness, not just a sales objection.
- GBA's weak point is criterion evidence. A new entrant that publishes even one well-run local criterion study (supervisor ratings, attendance, 90-day retention) would exceed the public evidence base of most game-based vendors.

### Gaps
- Could not verify in this session (blocked fetch): the Schmidt & Hunter 1998 comparison column, and the Sackett 2022 values for SJTs, assessment centres, integrity tests, conscientiousness and interests. From background knowledge, unverified here: Schmidt & Hunter 1998 gave work samples .54, cognitive ability .51, structured interviews .51; Sackett 2022 gives SJTs about .26 and assessment centres about .29. Check against Table 1 of the 2023 focal article before quoting.
- The primary SJT validity meta-analyses (McDaniel et al. 2007; Christian et al. 2010) and any simulation-specific meta-analysis were not retrieved.
- ITC Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests (2nd edition) and the ITC/ATP guidelines for technology-based assessment were not retrieved; their specific requirements should be sourced directly.
- No Nigerian or West African predictive validity study of an employment test was found, and no published Nigerian norm group for any commercial test. This is absence of search results after two queries, not proof of absence.
- No African subgroup-difference estimates for work samples or SJTs were found.
- No meta-analysis of GBA criterion validity against job performance was found for 2024–2026.

## 3. What buyers and candidates complain about

### Takeaway
The best-documented complaints are about process, not psychometrics: candidate resentment hit record highs in 2025 (15% in North America, 17% in EMEA) with non-communication the top theme, and only 38% of talent-acquisition leaders say they can show ROI on their technology, with integration (41%) and implementation cost (36%) the leading frustrations. On assessments specifically, the evidence is almost entirely vendor-produced and says device availability matters more than length: about one-third of mobile applicants abandon when an assessment is not available on their device, while shortening a test by 15 minutes adds only 1–2 points of completion.

### Cited Findings

**Candidate experience**
- [Industry benchmark; now run by Survale, formerly Talent Board] The 2025 CandE research is based on more than 66,000 completed candidate surveys collected 31 January–31 August 2025. Candidate resentment was 17% in EMEA, 15% in North America and 12% in both APAC and Latin America — record highs, and the first year EMEA exceeded North America. The top negative comment theme was lack of communication, ghosting and no follow-up; 31% of North American candidates reported not hearing back one to two or more months after applying — [Survale 2025 CandE key statistics](https://survale.com/2025-candidate-experience-cande-benchmark-research-key-statistics/); [2025 Survale CandE report PDF](https://survale.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-Survale-CandE-Research_The-Business-Impact-of-Candidate-Experience.pdf)
- [SECONDARY] Reasons for candidate withdrawal attributed to the 2024 CandE research: feeling their time was disrespected, processes taking too long, and lack of feedback — [ERE, 12 takeaways from the 2024 benchmark](https://ere.net/articles/12-key-takeaways-from-the-2024-candidate-experience-benchmark-research)
- [PEER working paper, field experiment] Even where AI interviewing improved outcomes, friction was measurable: in Jabarian & Henkel's Philippines study about 5% of applicants ended the interview on realising they were speaking to AI, the agent hit technical issues in about 7% of cases, and applicants rated the interaction as less "natural" — [Jabarian & Henkel, "Voice AI in Firms" (SSRN 5395709)](https://www.rivista.ai/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ssrn-5395709.pdf); [ERE summary](https://www.ere.net/articles/ai-outperformed-human-interviewers-in-a-new-field-study)

**Drop-off, length and device**
- [VENDOR, Dec 2020] Modern Hire: cutting 15 minutes from an assessment raised completion by only about 1–2 percentage points; over half of dropout occurs in the first 5–10 minutes; PC and tablet users complete at higher rates than mobile users "but only by several points"; approximately one-third of applicants applying on mobile phones drop out if the assessment is not available for their device type — [Modern Hire press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/modern-hire-reveals-new-research-on-applicant-dropout-and-assessment-completion-rates-301192559.html)
- [VENDOR, Oct 2025] HireVue's white paper repeats the pattern: assessment length was not a predictor of completion rates, with one assessment completing at almost 92% despite its length — [HireVue white paper, Oct 2025](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025_10_Applicant-Dropout-Completion-Rates_Whitepaper.pdf)
- [VENDOR, weak] Unsourced marketing figures in circulation: 72–84% completion for "optimised mobile programmes"; 70–85% for no-login mobile flows versus 20–50% for traditional mobile apply; a claimed 60% abandonment rate for traditional tests — [Hirevire](https://hirevire.com/articles/no-login-mobile-screening); [Test Partnership](https://www.testpartnership.com/blog/benefits-game-based-assessments-recruitment.html)

- [Analyst firm; exact report and date not confirmed] Aptitude Research: only 38% of talent-acquisition leaders can show ROI on their technology investments; one in three believe their current budgets are being wasted; top frustrations are lack of integration (41%) and cost of implementation (36%) — [Aptitude Research talent acquisition archive](https://www.aptituderesearch.com/tag/talent-acquisition/)
- [Analyst firm, 2025] Aptitude Research: only 28% are satisfied with their current ATS decision and 82% report significant functionality gaps; about one in four companies were replacing their ATS in 2025 — [Aptitude Research on SAP's acquisition of SmartRecruiters](https://www.aptituderesearch.com/sap-acquires-smartrecruiters-a-strategic-shift-to-agentic-ai/)

Detailed assessment findings are gated — [Criteria 2025–2026 Hiring Benchmark Report](https://www.criteriacorp.com/research/2025-2026-hiring-benchmark-report)
- [VENDOR survey, March 2026] Criteria's 2026 Candidate Experience Report (2,500+ job seekers) is framed around the résumé "losing its value in the age of AI" — [Criteria press release](https://www.criteriacorp.com/press-releases/2026/the-resume-losing-its-value-the-age-ai-annual-candidate-experience-report-finds)

### Inferences
- Completion is a design and device problem more than a length problem: most abandonment happens in the first 5–10 minutes and when the candidate's device is unsupported. For a mobile-only applicant population, "works fully on a low-end Android phone" is the single biggest lever, ahead of shaving minutes. All of this evidence is vendor-sourced and from North American and European pools, so it needs local replication.
- The dominant candidate grievance (silence after applying) is cheap to fix inside an assessment product through guaranteed outcome messages and feedback, and would be a visible differentiator.
- Buyers' core commercial objection is proof of value. With only 38% able to show ROI on talent technology, a venture that instruments outcomes (time-to-hire, 30- and 90-day retention, early performance) per client and reports them addresses the best-evidenced buyer complaint.
- "Skills-based hiring" adoption figures are inflated by self-report; half of organisations still say they cannot validate skills. The practical demand is for skills verification, which is what an assessment sells.

### Gaps
- No survey evidence was retrieved in this session on several complaints named in the brief: perceived job-irrelevance of tests, opaque AI scoring and candidate trust in AI evaluation, lack of local norms, and assessment price. Surveys known to exist on candidate trust in AI hiring (Gartner, Greenhouse, Pew) were not retrieved because the search budget ran out; do not cite figures for them from these notes.
- CandE data on assessments specifically (test length, relevance, feedback) was not retrieved; only the general resentment and communication findings.
- Josh Bersin Company and LinkedIn survey findings on assessment satisfaction were not retrieved.
- Criteria's detailed benchmark numbers (assessment usage, AI-cheating concern) are behind a form and were not obtained.
- No independent, peer-reviewed 2024–2026 study isolating mobile completion by assessment length was found; none at all for African candidate pools.
- The legal exposure of AI screening vendors in US litigation (for example the Workday collective action) was not researched in this session.

## 4. Emerging-market gaps: mobile-only candidates, data cost, low bandwidth, language and literacy, norms, identity fraud, informal work

### Takeaway
The strongest independent evidence for a Nigeria-first venture is economic, not psychometric: randomised trials in South Africa, Ethiopia and Uganda show that giving workseekers credible, shareable assessment results raises employment and earnings (South Africa: employment from 31% to 36% and weekly earnings up 34%; Ethiopia: earnings up 20% four years later at a cost of about USD 18 per person), because employers lack reliable skill signals. Nigerian exam bodies document industrial-scale impersonation, including 4,251 biometric-fraud cases and 190 AI image-morphing impersonations in the 2025 UTME, so identity assurance is a first-order design requirement that incumbent remote products built for other markets do not address.

### Cited Findings

**Skills signalling experiments in African labour markets**
- [PEER] Carranza, Garlick, Orkin & Rankin, "Job Search and Hiring with Limited Information about Workseekers' Skills", American Economic Review 112(11), 3547–3583 (2022). Field experiments with the Harambee Youth Employment Accelerator in Johannesburg covered 6,891 workseekers aged 18–29 (assessed September 2016–April 2017). Participants completed six assessments: numeracy, communication (verbal and written), grit, focus and planning. Results were reported as top, middle or bottom third relative to peers, not as raw scores — [AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20200961); [J-PAL evaluation summary](https://povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/impact-providing-information-about-job-seekers-skills-employment-south-africa); [World Bank guide to the certificate programme](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/230121635847848114/pdf/Guide-to-Job-Seeker-Skills-Certificate-Programme.pdf)
- [PEER] Results: certification that workseekers could credibly share with firms raised the employment rate from 31% to 36% (5 percentage points, a 17% increase) and weekly earnings by 34%. Giving the same information to workseekers only, without a credible certificate, changed beliefs and search similarly but had smaller effects on employment and earnings — showing that frictions sit on the firm side as well — [Oxford Mind and Behaviour Research Group summary](https://mbrg.bsg.ox.ac.uk/mind-and-behaviour-projects/impact-providing-information-about-job-seekers-skills-employment-south); [Harambee-hosted AER version](https://www.harambee.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/job-search-AER.pdf)
- [SECONDARY, J-PAL] Harambee built a modified version of the certification into its operations in late 2019 — [J-PAL evaluation page](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/print/pdf/node/2460)
- [PEER] Abebe, Caria, Fafchamps, Falco, Franklin & Quinn, "Anonymity or Distance? Job Search and Labour Market Exclusion in a Growing African City" (NBER WP 22409; randomised evaluation with 3,052 young job seekers in Addis Ababa). A job-application workshop that combined skills certification with CV, application and interview orientation had no effect on the probability of having a job, hours or earnings at eight months, though it raised the chance of permanent work. Four years later, being offered the workshop increased earnings by 20% and job satisfaction by 12%, with jobs lasting about 10% longer; the authors attribute the gain to better matching. An earlier draft quoted 25%, so the headline differs by version — [J-PAL evaluation summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/anonymity-or-distance-removing-obstacles-youth-employment-urban-ethiopia); [NBER w22409](https://nber.org/papers/w22409)
- [SECONDARY, policy brief] Cost-effectiveness of the Ethiopian workshop: an average wage gain of about USD 10 per month per worker for a one-off cost of USD 18.20 per individual — [GLM|LIC Policy Brief 22, May 2018](https://g2lm-lic.iza.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/GLMLIC-Policy-Brief_022-1.pdf)
- [PEER] Bassi & Nansamba, "Screening and Signalling Non-Cognitive Skills: Experimental Evidence from Uganda", Economic Journal 132(642), 471–511 (2022). Certificates of soft-skill grades were disclosed to both young workers and small-firm managers at job interviews. Managers and workers both updated beliefs; the result was more positive assortative matching and higher earnings conditional on employment. Lower-skill workers can be made worse off by disclosure — [Economic Journal record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/econjl/v132y2022i642p471-511..html); [J-PAL evaluation summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/impact-disclosing-soft-skills-certificates-recruitment-labor-market-outcomes-uganda)
- [SECONDARY, UNVERIFIED figures] An internal rate of return of 9–29% is reported for the Uganda certificates; one secondary source attributes an 8.7 percentage-point employment gain and 15% earnings gain to it, which I could not confirm in primary sources — [GiveWell soft skills certification review](https://www.givewell.org/international/technical/programs/soft-skills-certification)
- [SECONDARY, World Bank] ESTEEM: behavioural measures of socio-emotional skills outperform self-reports in predicting employment and income in Côte d'Ivoire, Nigeria and Tanzania — [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/what-skills-really-count--examining-socioemotional-skills-that-m)

**Mobile, data cost and bandwidth**
- [NGO case study, 2024–2025] Harambee's SA Youth platform was first built as a data-free site and later moved to zero-rating, under which mobile operators cover the data cost of youth usage. The employer portal is not subsidised, so employers pay their own data costs — [Harambee case study, "Unlocking Youth Employment in South Africa"](https://www.harambee.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Case-Study_South.Africa.9.2024_compressed.pdf)
- [NGO] After the 2020 switch to data-free access, youth engagement shifted towards working hours and away from late evening, when data bundles are cheaper — direct behavioural evidence that data cost governs when and whether candidates engage — [Harambee, "Inclusive by Design"](https://www.harambee.co.za/breaking-the-barriers-enabling-youth-employment-in-south-africa-through-digital-inclusion/)
- [SECONDARY] Harambee reports having supported 4 million young South Africans on SA Youth (headline only retrieved) — [Plumbing Africa](https://plumbingafrica.co.za/harambee-marks-milestone-of-supporting-4-million-young-south-africans-on-sa-youth-platform/)
- [VENDOR, non-African data] About one-third of mobile applicants drop out when an assessment is not available on their device — [Modern Hire, 2020](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/modern-hire-reveals-new-research-on-applicant-dropout-and-assessment-completion-rates-301192559.html)

**Identity fraud and proxy test-taking**
- [Press reporting official findings, May–June 2025] Nigeria's Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board (JAMB) reported from the 2025 UTME: 4,251 cases of biometric fraud; 244 cases of "WhatsApp runs"; impersonation at registration, double registration and attempted candidate substitution, often in collusion with some computer-based test centres; and over 3,000 candidates under investigation — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/featured/jamb-uncovers-4251-cases-of-biometric-fraud-in-2025-utme/); [Nairametrics, 9 May 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/05/09/utme-2025-jamb-uncovers-identity-biometric-fraud-and-244-cases-of-whatsapp-runs-across-nigeria/); [Nigeria Education News, 5 June 2025](https://thenigeriaeducationnews.com/2025/06/05/over-3000-candidates-under-investigation-for-digital-exam-fraud-in-2025-utme/)
- [Journal article on the official committee's findings; journal quality not assessed] The government-appointed special committee on the 2025 UTME found 190 instances of AI-assisted impersonation using image morphing; methods listed by the registrar included hacking of test centres, fingerprint manipulation, LAN extension schemes and "mercenary pairing" — [IJRTMR article on the 2025 UTME Special Committee investigation](https://www.ijrtmr.com/archives/biometric-fraud-syndicated-collusion-and-the-governance-of-technology-enabled-examination-malpractice-in-nigeria-evidence-from-the-2025-utme-special-committee-investigation)
- [Trade press, Dec 2024] The UK Nursing and Midwifery Council concluded that hundreds of nurses, registrants and applicants, had obtained their computer-based competency test fraudulently at a Nigerian test centre where proxies sat the test — evidence that supervised centres are not a sufficient control when the centre is compromised — [Nursing Times, 5 Dec 2024](https://www.nursingtimes.net/professional-regulation/nigerian-nurses-suffering-following-nmc-fraud-allegations-05-12-2024/)
- [SECONDARY] The Nigeria Customs Service warned of a syndicate running fake recruitment portals and forged test schedules aimed at job seekers — [Streamline Feed](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/nigeria-customs-exposes-high-level-syndicate-running-fake-recruitment-portals)

**Other emerging-market evidence on automated assessment at volume**
- [PEER working paper, 2025] Jabarian & Henkel, "Voice AI in Firms": 70,884 applications to 48 entry-level customer-service postings in the Philippines (recruiter PSG Global Solutions), randomised to a human interviewer, an AI voice agent, or a choice. AI-interviewed applicants received offers in 9.73% of cases against 8.7% for human-interviewed (about 12% more), were 18% more likely to start work and 17% more likely to be employed after 30 days, with no decline in productivity of hires. Given the choice, 78% chose the AI interviewer. Final hiring decisions stayed with human recruiters in all arms. Counts differ slightly across sources (70,000; 70,884 applications; 67,056 randomised) — [SSRN 5395709](https://www.rivista.ai/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ssrn-5395709.pdf); [Chicago Booth Review podcast](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/podcast/are-ai-interviews-better)
- [GOV/IO, Nov 2025] A World Bank working paper analyses online job vacancies in South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya and Uganda and finds demand for transversal skills; it does not test signalling or assessment — [ERIC record, World Bank Education Working Paper No. 21](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED679812)

### Inferences
- The economic case for assessment in African labour markets is unusually well evidenced: three RCTs in three countries show that credible, portable skill signals improve matching. Two design lessons recur. First, the signal must be credible to the employer (a certificate from a trusted third party), not just informative to the candidate. Second, coarse relative bands (thirds) were enough to move outcomes, which lowers the measurement precision needed for a first product.
- A candidate-owned, verifiable credential is a defensible differentiator against incumbents whose results belong to the employer and die with the application. It also brings a known side-effect to manage: disclosure can hurt lower-scoring workers (Uganda).
- Identity assurance must be designed for collusion at the venue and for biometric spoofing, not just for a lone candidate with a chatbot. The JAMB and NMC cases show both centre-level collusion and AI-morphed identities in Nigeria. Remote proctoring imported from other markets assumes bandwidth and a webcam on a laptop, which mobile-only candidates lack.
- Data cost visibly shapes engagement (Harambee's time-of-day shift). Zero-rating or data-light delivery, plus an employer-pays model, is the proven pattern; an assessment that needs sustained video upload works against it.
- Voice AI interviewing has been shown to work at scale in an emerging-market frontline labour pool (Philippines call centres), with candidates preferring it. Voice also sidesteps some reading load, which matters for lower-literacy pools, though the study does not test that.

### Gaps
- No statistics were retrieved for Nigerian smartphone ownership, data prices or the 2025 tariff changes, nor for the share of Nigerian job seekers who are mobile-only; the search budget ended before that query ran.
- No research was found on multilingual or low-literacy assessment delivery (Pidgin, Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo; audio or pictorial formats) in hiring.
- No African norm-group data, published or vendor, was found for any frontline assessment.
- Nothing was retrieved on Generation (Kenya/Nigeria) assessment practice, on IPA studies beyond those above, or on assessments for informal-sector work.
- No evidence was found on proxy test-taking rates in Nigerian private-sector recruitment tests specifically; the Nigerian evidence is from admissions and licensing exams, plus fake-portal scams.
- Whether Harambee's online assessments are themselves zero-rated, and how they perform on low bandwidth, was not confirmed.

## 5. Regulatory and standards landscape for hiring assessment

### Takeaway
The EU's high-risk obligations for employment AI were pushed from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027 by the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force July 2026), while New York City's bias-audit law was judged ineffectively enforced by the State Comptroller in December 2025; neither creates near-term pressure on a Nigerian operator. Locally, Nigeria's NDPA 2023 section 37 gives candidates a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions and the GAID 2025 (effective 19 September 2025) adds consent, bias-safeguard and DPIA duties, and South Africa removed the HPCSA certification requirement from the Employment Equity Act on 1 January 2025 but HPCSA classification of psychological tests still applies.

### Cited Findings

**EU AI Act after the Digital Omnibus**
- [LAW] Council and Parliament reached a provisional agreement on the AI Digital Omnibus on 7 May 2026. Obligations for stand-alone high-risk systems in Annex III (which covers employment, alongside biometrics, credit scoring, education and critical infrastructure) move from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027; obligations for AI embedded in Annex I regulated products move to 2 August 2028 — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Cloud Security Alliance research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/); [DLA Piper employment update](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act)

### Inferences
- A Nigerian product should be built so that a human makes or can review every adverse hiring decision, with consent capture, a contest route and a DPIA on file. That satisfies NDPA section 37 and the GAID, and the same design answers Kenya's section 35 and the EU Article 22 lineage. This is a low-cost design choice now and an expensive retrofit later.
- No African jurisdiction covered here imposes a bias-audit or technical-validity requirement on hiring assessments. Voluntary publication of validity and adverse-impact evidence would therefore be a differentiator, not a compliance cost, and would pre-position the venture for EU-style buyers when Annex III obligations bite in December 2027.
- South Africa is the one African market with a profession-based gate: tests of psychological constructs need HPCSA classification and registered users. Positioning an instrument as a job-knowledge or work-sample measure, not a psychological test, changes the regulatory route there — a point for legal advice, not a conclusion.
- With BPS reviews paused and HPCSA certification gone, independent third-party test review is currently hard to obtain in two of the reference jurisdictions. That raises the value of transparent technical manuals and independent academic validation.
- NYC's experience shows that disclosure-based AI-hiring rules are weakly enforced; buyer procurement questionnaires, not regulators, are the practical driver of audit demand.

## 6. Trend lines: skills-based hiring, conversational AI interviewers, LLM scoring, consolidation, and incumbents in Africa

### Takeaway
The clearest structural trend is that frontline, conversational hiring is being absorbed into HR suites — Workday bought Paradox for about USD 1.0 billion (announced 21 August 2025, closed 1 October 2025) — while a large randomised trial shows AI voice interviewers matching or beating human screeners on offers, starts and 30-day retention for entry-level service roles. LLM scoring is spreading faster than its validation: documented scoring biases in LLM judges exist and a best-practice literature is only now forming. I found no evidence in this session of a global incumbent making a dedicated move into Nigeria or mobile-first African delivery.

### Inferences
- Frontline conversational screening is becoming a feature of global HR suites. Competing on chat-based screening and scheduling alone is a weak position; the suites do not, however, bring local norms, identity assurance for the Nigerian context, data-light delivery, or candidate-owned credentials.
- The Philippines trial is the best available evidence that AI-led first-round interviewing is acceptable to, and even preferred by, entry-level candidates in an emerging market, provided a human keeps the final decision — which also aligns with NDPA section 37.
- LLM scoring should be treated as an unvalidated instrument until locally validated: documented prompt- and rubric-order effects mean two deployments of the "same" scoring can differ. Deterministic behavioural scoring from simulation telemetry, with LLM scoring confined to audited sub-tasks, is the lower-risk architecture, and it also defuses the "opaque AI scoring" objection.
- Demand is for skills verification, not skills rhetoric: adoption claims far outrun actual hiring change, and half of employers say they cannot validate skills.
- Consolidation creates orphaned mid-market and regional buyers as products are folded into suites, and incumbents' attention is on agentic features for enterprise buyers in developed markets — an opening at the regional, frontline end.
