## 1. Psychometric and behavioural credit scoring for thin-file borrowers (LenddoEFL, Begini, Coremetrix, others): what rigorous studies found, gaming, and use in Nigeria

### Takeaway
Independent evidence (Peru, Ethiopia) says psychometric credit scores work as a *supplement*: they lower risk as a second screen for already-banked borrowers and can open credit to people without histories at similar repayment, but they have limited power, and may raise risk, when used to rescue applicants a conventional score rejected. The category's pioneer, LenddoEFL, appears to have wound down around 2021, which is the main cautionary tale; Begini is the live player with a Nigerian footprint, and no independent validation of it was found.

### Cited Findings

**Peru (EFL tool) — the first external study**
- [INDEPENDENT] Arráiz, Bruhn and Stucchi tested the Entrepreneurial Finance Lab (EFL) psychometric tool for screening small-business borrowers in Peru. The paper describes itself as the first external study of psychometric credit scoring, using independently collected repayment data and no EFL-affiliated co-author. Issued as IDB-WP-625 (October 2015) and World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 7506 (December 2015); published in *The World Bank Economic Review* vol. 30, supplement, pp. S67–S76 (2017). — [IDB working paper](https://publications.iadb.org/publications/english/document/Psychometrics-as-a-Tool-to-Improve-Screening-and-Access-to-Credit.pdf); [WBER record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/wbecrv/v30y2017isupplement_1ps67-s76..html); [World Bank Open Knowledge](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/89709396-e718-5421-8725-5a30f19d0d37/full)
- [INDEPENDENT] Finding 1: the psychometric test "can lower the risk of the loan portfolio when used as a secondary screening mechanism for already banked entrepreneurs". Finding 2: for entrepreneurs with no credit history, the EFL tool "can increase access to credit without increasing portfolio risk". — [IDB working paper](https://publications.iadb.org/publications/english/document/Psychometrics-as-a-Tool-to-Improve-Screening-and-Access-to-Credit.pdf)
- [INDEPENDENT] Caution from the authors' own World Bank blog summary: when used to find low-risk applicants *among those rejected by the traditional method*, the EFL tool "has limited power and could even lead to an increase in risk" (extract truncated at that point). — [World Bank All About Finance blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/allaboutfinance/psychometrics-tool-improve-screening-and-access-credit)
- [INDEPENDENT] Context that limits transfer to Nigeria: in Peru private credit bureaus cover 100 percent of adults, so everybody has a conventional score to compare against. — [World Bank All About Finance blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/allaboutfinance/psychometrics-tool-improve-screening-and-access-credit)
- [INDEPENDENT] Follow-up with a large Peruvian bank (Arráiz, Bruhn, Ruiz Ortega, Stucchi, World Bank PRWP 8276, December 2017, "Are Psychometric Tools a Viable Screening Method for Small and Medium-Size Enterprise Lending?"): applicants above a score threshold were offered a loan. For applicants *with* a credit history the tool did not increase SME loan use; for those *without* a history, a search extract reports SME loan use rose 54 percentage points without worse repayment, and that the extra borrowing came mainly from non-partner lenders. The 54-point figure came through a search summary and needs checking against the PDF. — [WPS 8276](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/729911513262009360/pdf/WPS8276.pdf)
- [INDEPENDENT] The same author group later published "Borrower leakage from costly screening" (*Journal of Development Economics*, 2021, with Benjamin Roth), on the pattern where the screening lender pays for the test and other lenders pick up the borrower. Title and venue only; findings not read. — [WPS 8276 search record](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/729911513262009360/pdf/WPS8276.pdf)

**Ethiopia (psychometric score as a substitute for collateral)**
- [INDEPENDENT] Alibhai, Cassidy, Goldstein and Papineni, "Evening the Credit Score? Impact of Psychometric Loan Appraisal for Women Entrepreneurs", World Bank PRWP 10230 (November 2022; Gender Innovation Lab version March 2023). Randomised controlled trial with an Ethiopian microfinance institution (Wasasa), testing psychometric scoring instead of collateral for individual-liability loans up to USD 7,500, advertised through the World Bank's Women Entrepreneurship Development Project. — [RePEc record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wbk:wbrwps:10230); [Open Knowledge](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/39998)
- [INDEPENDENT] Sample: 357 women took the test and baseline survey between April 2018 and March 2020; 259 scored above the cut-off; 324 completed the endline in September 2021. 92% lived or worked in Adama, 8% in Asela. — [World Bank Microdata Library](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/5793); [Evening the Credit Score PDF](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/422032ab83170e5b63cbf21753e6829c-0050022022/original/Evening-the-Credit-Score.pdf)
- [INDEPENDENT] Results as abstracted: positive impacts on women's access to credit and on firm survival through COVID-19 and conflict; the psychometric-appraised, uncollateralised loans showed "similarly high repayment rates to regular collateralized loans offered by the MFI prior to the onset of COVID-19". Effect sizes were not in the extracts. — [RePEc record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wbk:wbrwps:10230)
- [INDEPENDENT] Instrument: an interactive tablet test of games, puzzles and questions; the scoring model was trained on answers and repayment data from other borrowers in pilots in Ethiopia and neighbouring countries; the technology "was provided by a US company" (not named in the extract). — [Evening the Credit Score PDF](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/422032ab83170e5b63cbf21753e6829c-0050022022/original/Evening-the-Credit-Score.pdf)
- [INDEPENDENT] An earlier World Bank note on psychometrics in Ethiopia (2018) exists but was not readable in this session. — [World Bank 2018 note](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/651751524666164146/pdf/125731-WP-PsychometricsEthiopiafinal.pdf)

**Gaming**
- [SECONDARY] A World Economic Forum article on personality tests in lending has a "Gaming the system" section and notes borrowers "would likely give the answers they think reflect better credit trustworthiness". — [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/stories/financial-and-monetary-systems/could-personality-tests-help-banks-make-loan-decisions/)
- [INDEPENDENT, hiring not credit] Faking rises with stakes: a 2021 study of real applicants found more socially desirable answers at higher stakes; a publisher overview cites 30–50% of applicants having misrepresented themselves at some point; reviews find faking can reorder rankings, and that many detection methods are less reliable than hoped. Suggested countermeasures: forced-choice formats, neutralised items, complementary data. — [Hogrefe overview](https://www.hogrefe.com/group/news/faking-personality-assessment); [ITB review, 2026](https://itb-academic-tests.org/en/2026/07/18/does-faking-make-personality-assessments-useless-in-higher-education-admissions/)

### Inferences
- The independent credit evidence maps directly onto hiring: a behavioural score earned its keep as a *second screen layered on existing data*, and was weakest when asked to overrule a conventional screen. A frontline assessment sold as "replace your CV screen and interview" is the riskier claim; "add to your existing funnel and cut early attrition" is the claim the neighbouring evidence supports.
- The Ethiopia design (model trained on local repayment outcomes) and the Standard Bank experience both say the asset is the local outcome dataset, not the questionnaire. That is the same moat a Nigerian hiring assessment would build with retention and performance outcomes.
- LenddoEFL had Harvard origins, World Bank and IDB studies, and a tier-one African bank, and still appears to have failed commercially. Credible science did not protect it. The "borrower leakage" finding suggests one reason: the buyer who pays for screening does not capture all the benefit. The hiring analogue is a candidate assessed by one employer and hired by another, which argues for candidate-portable results or per-hire pricing.
- Credit scoring is a plausible adjacent market only if the venture's tasks measure something stable (conscientiousness, numeracy, impulse control) and it can get repayment labels. Begini already occupies that position with lenders, so this is more likely a partnership or a threat than a first expansion.

### Gaps
- Exact effect sizes for Peru (arrears differences, sample sizes) and Ethiopia were not retrievable; the "54 percentage points" figure needs checking. A widely repeated claim that high scorers in an early Ethiopia pilot were "seven times" more likely to repay could not be found in any extract. [UNVERIFIED]
- No published study of gaming or coaching specifically on EFL or any psychometric credit score was found. EFL's public descriptions of its anti-gaming design were not retrieved.
- LenddoEFL's legal status in 2022–2026 (dissolution, asset sale) is unconfirmed; the only evidence is Wikipedia.
- No evidence of any EFL or LenddoEFL deployment in Nigeria was found. No independent validation of Begini, and no named Nigerian Begini clients, were found.
- A Philippines study reporting that personality measures were not strong predictors of default was mentioned in one search summary without a traceable citation. [UNVERIFIED]
- Juvo, Tala, Branch internal models and Pngme's current status: the one search returned nothing relevant, and the budget ran out before a retry.

## 2. Agent and gig-worker vetting and onboarding risk in Africa; identity and proctoring vendors and their pricing

### Takeaway
Agent vetting in Nigeria is identity, credit and location control, not behavioural assessment: the Central Bank of Nigeria's October 2025 guidelines push principals toward BVN checks, credit checks, geo-tagging and one-principal exclusivity, and no source found shows any network using an integrity or behavioural test. Fraud is large and rising (NIBSS-attributed industry losses of N52.26 billion in 2024), and identity checks are priced in cents to low dollars per check, which sets a hard price anchor for anything sold into agent onboarding.

### Cited Findings

**Regulation and how agents are vetted**
- [INDEPENDENT] The CBN issued "Guidelines for the Operations of Agent Banking in Nigeria" on 6 October 2025, superseding all earlier agent-banking and super-agent rules. — [CBN circular and guidelines PDF](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2025/CCD/CIRCULAR%20AND%20GUIDELINES%20FOR%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20AGENT%20BANKING%20IN%20NIGERIA%20OCTOBER%206%202025.pdf)
- [SECONDARY] Nigeria has about 2 million banking agents, who must choose one principal (for example Moniepoint, OPay or PalmPay) by 1 April 2026 under the exclusivity rule. — [TechCabal, 7 Oct 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/07/cbn-bans-agents-owning-multiple-moniepoint-opay-palmpay-terminals/)
- [SECONDARY] Qualification rules bar debtors and people with blacklisted BVNs from acting as agents; principals must build credit checks, BVN verification and legal clearances into onboarding, and monitor agents' BVNs for activity outside designated accounts. — [Nairametrics, 7 Oct 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/10/07/cbn-bars-debtors-blacklisted-bvns-from-operating-as-pos-agents/)
- [SECONDARY] Principals must conduct "comprehensive due diligence" and risk assessment before appointing agents; board members face personal financial penalties for compliance failures; individuals may be appointed directly as agents but may not run sub-agent networks. — [Chambers regulatory update](https://chambers.com/articles/regulatory-update-key-legal-and-compliance-changes-in-the-cbn-guidelines-for-the-operation); [Mondaq analysis](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/financial-services/1701568/an-analysis-of-the-guidelines-for-operations-of-agent-banking-in-nigeria)
- [SECONDARY] Other controls: daily cash-out capped at N1.2 million per agent and N100,000 per customer; dedicated agent accounts or wallets; daily transaction reports to NIBSS and monthly reports to the CBN; POS terminals geo-tagged (directive of 25 August 2025, deadline 20 October 2025) with agents kept within 10 metres of the registered location; sanctions include blacklisting, licence suspension and fines. — [Channels TV, 7 Oct 2025](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/10/07/cbn-pegs-pos-agents-daily-transactions-at-₦1-2m/); [Nairametrics, 6 Oct 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/10/06/cbn-directs-banks-to-submit-monthly-reports-on-pos-agents-activities); [TechCabal, 27 Aug 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/08/27/cbn-10-metre-rule-pos-agents/)
- [SECONDARY, low quality] Moniepoint agent onboarding as described by third-party guides: BVN, utility bill, CAC documents for registered businesses, contact from an aggregator within 48 hours, device collected through a Moniepoint office and cluster manager. Guides conflict on details and none mentions a guarantor. Moniepoint's own support pages cover account KYC tiers, not agent vetting. — [Entrepreneurs.ng guide](https://entrepreneurs.ng/?p=32109); [Moniepoint KYC levels](https://support.moniepoint.com/topics/what-are-the-requirements-for-each-kyc-level-on-moniepoint-113)
- [SECONDARY] Agent termination risk runs the other way too: a report headlined "Undefined Targets Expose Moniepoint Agents To Arbitrary Termination" (headline only; article not read). — [The Whistler](https://thewhistler.ng/undefined-targets-expose-moniepoint-agents-to-arbitrary-termination/)
- [SECONDARY] The agent association (AMMAN/AMMBAN) asked the CBN to review implementation timelines, citing network reliability and job losses. — [bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/nigeria-s-cbn-bans-multi-operator-pos-model-to-streamline-agent-banking-405273)

**Fraud and what it costs**
- [SECONDARY, NIBSS-attributed] Nigerian financial institutions lost N52.26 billion to fraud in 2024, up from N17.67 billion in 2023. This is all-channel fraud, not agent fraud alone. — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/?p=2895783); 2023 figure also in [AllAfrica, 1 Oct 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202510010339.html)
- [SECONDARY, weak] Fraud attempts through agent channels reportedly rose from N9 billion in 2021 to over N22 billion in 2023 (attributed to NIBSS by a low-quality secondary source; verify before use). — [MEXC news](https://www.mexc.com/news/246780)
- [INDEPENDENT] Innovations for Poverty Action's 2024 Nigeria consumer-protection survey of active digital-finance users (not nationally representative): 58% had been targeted by a phone or SMS fraud attempt; 6% had lost money as a result of a challenge; agent overcharging is one of the challenge types recorded. — [IPA 2024 Nigeria report](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/2025-01/2024-Nigeria-Report-Consumer-Protection-in-Digital-Financial-Services.pdf)
- [SECONDARY] Agents are often the victims: common schemes are social engineering, fraudulent reversal claims and manipulated transaction confirmations; an agent-association chairman said "the agent becomes the victim" in disputes. — [National Daily](https://nationaldailyng.com/nigerias-pos-banking-boom-faces-rising-fraud-threats-as-agents-bear-growing-risks/amp/)
- [SECONDARY] GSMA data reported in March 2026: identity fraud affected 90% of mobile money providers and social engineering 88%. — [WeeTracker, 30 Mar 2026](https://weetracker.com/2026/03/30/mobile-money-africa-inactivity-gsma-report-2026/)

- [SECONDARY] Paystack publishes an identity verification pricing page (figures not extracted). — [Paystack support](https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2130818)

### Inferences
- No source found shows any Nigerian agent network using behavioural or integrity assessment at onboarding. That is an open space, but the absence is itself evidence: networks onboard at very high volume through aggregators, at near-zero unit cost, and the regulator's definition of due diligence is identity, credit and location. A behavioural product would be judged against a database lookup costing N10–N60, or roughly USD 0.06–0.35.
- The October 2025 guidelines create the one plausible opening: boards are personally exposed for agent due diligence and exclusivity makes each agent a longer-lived relationship from April 2026, so principals have more reason to select agents instead of simply registering them. Whether "enhanced due diligence" could be read to include a suitability or integrity screen depends on Section 7 of the guidelines, which was not read.
- Much reported agent-channel fraud is committed *against* agents (reversal scams, counterfeit cash). A screening product addresses agent-perpetrated fraud only; fraud-awareness training simulations may fit the actual loss pattern better than pre-onboarding integrity tests.
- Identity vendors (Smile ID, Dojah, Youverify, Prembly) already own the onboarding API slot and the buyer relationship. They are more likely distribution partners or eventual bundlers than customers.

### Gaps
- Section 7 (agent qualification and due diligence) and Section 8 (appointment) of the CBN guidelines were not read; the training requirement and the exact scope of principal liability for agent misconduct are unconfirmed.
- OPay, PalmPay and FirstMonie agent requirements, and whether any network demands guarantors: not searched (budget exhausted). M-Pesa (Safaricom) agent vetting: not searched.
- No agent-level churn, dormancy or agent-perpetrated fraud rate was found from EFInA, SANEF, NIBSS or the networks. The N52.26 billion figure is all fraud, all channels.
- Sumsub pricing and any Africa-specific proctoring vendors or prices: not found or not searched.
- Gig-worker vetting (ride-hailing, delivery) in Africa: not searched.

## 3. Contact-centre training simulation and AI role-play vendors moving into pre-hire assessment (and pre-hire vendors moving into training)

### Takeaway
The crossover is already happening from the training side: Zenarate sells pre-hire simulation after acquiring Bright, and Second Nature markets candidate scoring to HR. These vendors bring the simulation engine but not selection science; a SHRM piece notes most such tools are not "performance validated". No evidence was found of the reverse move by pre-hire vendors into training.

### Inferences
- The same generative role-play engine now serves training and hiring, so the product boundary between "simulation for onboarding" and "simulation for selection" is disappearing. What separates the two is validation evidence, adverse-impact evidence and defensible scoring, which is exactly the credibility infrastructure covered in sections 6–7.
- For a Nigerian venture this cuts both ways: training is a natural second product (same content, same buyer, recurring use), and a global training vendor could add a hiring module for Nigerian BPOs with little effort. Local norms, accents, connectivity constraints and outcome data are what a global entrant would lack.

### Gaps
- SymTrain, Attensi and ETU: the one search returned nothing on them and the budget ran out before a retry. Their pre-hire positioning is unknown from this session.
- No evidence found of pre-hire vendors (Harver, HireVue, SHL, HiringBranch) launching training products. Absence of evidence from one search, not a confirmed negative.
- Zenarate/Bright deal date and terms; any independent validation of training vendors' hiring scores.

## 4. Integrity tests: evidence base and vendors

### Takeaway
The evidence for integrity tests is contested and the contest is about who ran the studies: the classic estimate for predicting job performance is .41 (Ones, Viswesvaran & Schmidt, 1993), an independent re-analysis put it at .15 and found publisher-run studies gave .27 against .12 for non-publisher studies (Van Iddekinge et al., 2012), and the current reference table sits at .31 (Sackett et al., 2022). For a new entrant the lesson is that publisher-only validity evidence is discounted by more than half.

### Cited Findings
- [INDEPENDENT] Ones, Viswesvaran & Schmidt (1993, *Journal of Applied Psychology*): based on 665 validity coefficients, estimated mean operational predictive validity of integrity tests for supervisory ratings of job performance is .41; validities positive across settings. — [University of Minnesota record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/comprehensive-meta-analysis-of-integrity-test-validities-findings/)
- [INDEPENDENT] Van Iddekinge, Roth, Raymark & Odle-Dusseau (2012, *Journal of Applied Psychology* 97(3), 499–530): 104 studies (134 samples), split roughly evenly between publisher and non-publisher authors. Job performance validity .15 corrected for criterion unreliability (.12 observed); about .18 with an indirect range-restriction correction. By author type: .27 for studies by integrity test publishers versus .12 for non-publishers. — [University of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-Criterion-Related-Validity-of-Integrity-Tests/9984380535302771)
- [INDEPENDENT] The rebuttals: Ones, Viswesvaran & Schmidt argued the 2012 database was partial and included scales that are not integrity tests; Van Iddekinge et al. replied that only about 30% of the accessible primary studies from the 1993 meta-analysis met their inclusion criteria; test publishers (Harris et al.) raised coding concerns; Sackett & Schmitt concluded exclusion criteria, artifact corrections and sampling error were unlikely to explain the gap, and that neither side documented its effect sizes well enough to resolve it. — [Ones et al. reply record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/integrity-tests-predict-counterproductive-work-behaviors-and-job-/); [Sackett & Schmitt, "On Reconciling Conflicting Meta-Analytic Findings"](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/371132)
- [INDEPENDENT] Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens (2022, *Journal of Applied Psychology* 107(11), 2040–2068) revised the standard validity table by dropping unjustified range-restriction corrections; top procedures stay near the top but mean validities fall by roughly .10–.20. Values reproduced in the 2023 follow-up table (Schmidt & Hunter 1998 → Sackett 2022): integrity tests .41 → .31; empirically keyed biodata .35 → .38; work sample tests .54 → .33; general mental ability .51 → .31. Structured interviews emerge as possibly the strongest predictor. — [Sackett et al. 2023, *Industrial and Organizational Psychology*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/core-reader); [SMU record of the 2022 paper](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6894); [SIOP TIP summary](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance)
- [INDEPENDENT] In the same table the extracted rows end with values that appear to be Black–White subgroup differences (d): integrity .10, biodata .33, work samples .67, general mental ability .79. Column labels were not visible in the extract, so treat the interpretation as probable, not confirmed. — [Sackett et al. 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/core-reader)
- [INDEPENDENT] A published commentary on the 2022 revision argues that convincing evidence for the predictive validity of integrity tests in practical settings "does not exist". Which commentary carried the line was not pinned. — [Commentary: "Rearranging the deck chairs"](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3B9489913DA301DD2A95605F9E54FEB2/S1754942623000329a.pdf/rearranging-the-deck-chairs-on-the-titanic-what-are-practitioners-to-do.pdf); [Commentary: "Time to end the horse race"](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E0A71A70C4C860B324399076B774F46F/S1754942623000275a.pdf/interpreting-validity-evidence-it-is-time-to-end-the-horse-race.pdf)
- [SELF-CLAIM] South African integrity-testing supply sits with background-screening firms: iFacts is the provider of an integrity test from INTEG and says integrity assessments must meet Employment Equity Act standards. — [Lionesses of Africa, 1 May 2025](https://www.lionessesofafrica.com/blog/2025/05/01/are-you-using-a-reliable-integrity-tool-for-your-hiring-process)
- [SECONDARY] South African background screening is a scaled adjacent industry: MIE's Background Screening Index reports over 3.2 million checks. — [MIE news](https://www.mie.co.za/news/tag/bsi/)

### Inferences
- The .27 versus .12 publisher gap is the most useful single number for a new assessment publisher: buyers and reviewers who know the literature will discount vendor-run validation by half or more, so one independently run or co-authored study is worth several internal ones.
- Work samples fell from .54 to .33 in the revised table, with an apparent subgroup difference of .67. A simulation-based venture should not quote the old ".54, best predictor" line; sophisticated buyers will know it is superseded, and the subgroup figure means adverse-impact monitoring needs to be designed in from the start.
- Integrity testing is an adjacent product for cash-handling roles (tellers, agents, retail), and its low apparent subgroup difference is attractive, but the practical-settings evidence is disputed and faking is the standing objection. Bundling a short integrity component inside a simulation is lower risk than selling a stand-alone integrity test.

### Gaps
- Structured interview (.42), job knowledge and conscientiousness values in Sackett 2022 were not confirmed in extracts. The ".34" figure in the brief was not found; it may refer to a different criterion in the 1993 paper. [UNVERIFIED]
- Counterproductive work behaviour validities (the criterion integrity tests are built for) from either meta-analysis were not extracted.
- Giotto, IP200, Veris and Merchants: the vendor search was cut off by the budget. One earlier extract showed an "Integrity Profiles 200 (IP200)" price of R1,400 excluding VAT per online test on a South African price list, but the source page could not be pinned. [UNVERIFIED] Publisher, HPCSA classification status and validity evidence for each remain unresearched.
- No integrity-test vendor or usage data for Nigeria was found.

## 5. Substitutes buyers use instead of assessment (referrals, staffing and employer-of-record firms, academies and bootcamps, probation, guarantors)

### Takeaway
The strongest documented substitutes are guarantors (a liability transfer, standard in Nigerian hiring) and work-readiness intermediaries that assess, train and place (Harambee at multi-million scale in South Africa, ALX in Nigeria). The best causal evidence in the whole field belongs to a substitute: Harambee's assessment-and-certificate RCT in the *American Economic Review* shows that assessment results the candidate can show to firms raise employment and earnings.

### Cited Findings

**Guarantors and fidelity cover**
- [SECONDARY, commercial guide] "Most Nigerian employers require new hires to submit one or two employment guarantor letters"; the guarantor vouches for character and accepts responsibility for loss the employee causes. — [Awajis guarantor-letter guide](https://awajis.com/?p=53583)
- [SECONDARY] Bank staff unions described a directive under which a financial guarantor must earn twice the annual income of the employee guaranteed (issuer, date and scope not in the extract). — [Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/?p=164154)
- [SECONDARY, legal] Some Nigerian employers insist on fidelity guarantees for staff in sensitive positions; cover can be named, group or blanket (for example all cash handlers). — [Mondaq, legal nature of bonds in Nigeria](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/securities/756142/the-legal-nature-of-bonds-in-commercial-transactions-in-nigeria); [Wikipedia: fidelity bond](https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Fidelity_bond)
- [SECONDARY, opinion] A legal commentary argues the employer, not the guarantor, carries the burden in work-related arrests. — [TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/employer-not-guarantor-bears-the-burden-of-work-related-arrests)

**Academies, bootcamps and work-readiness intermediaries**
- [INDEPENDENT] Carranza, Garlick, Orkin & Rankin, "Job Search and Hiring with Limited Information about Workseekers' Skills", *American Economic Review* 112(11), 3547–3583 (2022), run with Harambee in South Africa. Giving workseekers assessment results they can credibly share with firms raises employment and earnings. Giving the information only to workseekers moves beliefs and search similarly but has much smaller employment and earnings effects (one extract: no employment effect, some earnings effect). Giving it only to firms raises callbacks and interview invitations. Effect sizes were not in the extracts. — [AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20200961); [Harvard CID working paper 383, July 2020](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/cid/publications/faculty-working-papers/job-search-and-hiring); [IZA DP 14529](https://iza.org/publications/dp/14529/imprint); [Harambee summary](https://www.harambee.co.za/job-search-and-hiring-with-two-sided-limited-information-about-workseekers-skills/)
- [SELF-CLAIM] Harambee Youth Employment Accelerator, founded 2011 (CEO Kasthuri Soni): homepage as updated 11 December 2025 lists 4.6 million work-seekers supported, 1.6 million-plus connected to earning opportunities, 3,000-plus employer partners and R45.2 billion of youth income. Its impact page gives 1.7 million-plus and 3,500-plus; a Clinton Foundation listing gives 4.5 million supported and 1.8 million opportunities. The figures differ by page and date. It is an anchor partner of the SA Youth network (sayouth.mobi) and calls itself South Africa's largest youth employment programme. — [Harambee homepage](https://www.harambee.co.za/); [Harambee impact](https://www.harambee.co.za/impact/); [Co-Impact profile](https://co-impact.org/program-partners/harambee-youth-employment-accelerator/); [Clinton Foundation](https://www.clintonfoundation.org/?p=107333)
- [SELF-CLAIM] ALX: 347,100 graduates across Africa, 257,900 transitioned into work, 63% of graduates employed within six months (continent-wide, self-reported, no independent verification). Access Bank, Stanbic, Absa, KPMG, Deloitte, EY, MTN and others are each said to employ 50–180 ALX graduates. — [TechTrends KE, 25 May 2026](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/05/25/alx-reports-347000-graduates-as-african-ai-market-heads-to-16-5bn/); [ALX press](https://www.alxafrica.com/news-press/alx-builds-africas-workforce-as-a-competitor-in-the-ai-driven-global-economy/)
- [SELF-CLAIM] ALX Nigeria says it reached nearly 100,000 Nigerian youths in 2024, with nearly half a million applications, and that over 12,000 learners completed programmes in one six-month period in 2024; placement outcomes for Nigeria were not reported. — [Nairametrics, 21 Jan 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/01/21/alx-nigeria-empowers-nearly-100000-nigerian-youths-and-wins-10-awards-in-2024); [Techeconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/alx-nigeria-moves-more-than-12000-youths-closer-to-jobs-in-six-months)

### Inferences
- A guarantor is not a predictor; it shifts loss to a third party after the fact and screens only for social capital. An assessment does not replace it for cash-handling roles, so the realistic position is "alongside guarantors, and a fairer filter than who-you-know". Fidelity insurers are a possible channel if assessment data can be shown to reduce claims.
- The AER result is the best external argument for a *candidate-held, portable* result: the employment gain came from results workseekers could show firms. That supports a two-sided design (employer-paid screening plus a candidate certificate) and partly answers the "leakage" problem seen in credit scoring.
- Intermediaries that bundle sourcing, assessment, training and placement are the strongest substitute because the employer buys an outcome (a job-ready hire) and never sees assessment as a line item. They are also the most natural partners: they need assessment and rarely build it rigorously.

### Gaps
- Not searched (budget exhausted): Nigerian staffing and outsourcing firms and employer-of-record providers as substitutes; share of bank and telco frontline staff hired through outsourcers; use of probation periods as a screening device; prevalence and measured quality of referral hiring in Nigeria.
- Generation Nigeria: no results. Harambee's work-readiness assessment content and whether employers pay for it: not found.
- Effect sizes for the AER study were not retrieved.
- The guarantor evidence is from commercial guides and press; no survey of how many Nigerian employers require guarantors, or for which roles, was found.

## 6. How niche specialists built authority: assets each built

### Takeaway
Across eight cases the pattern is consistent: a narrow, defensible claim; documentation a sceptic can read (technical manuals, a study archive, a published audit or RCT); a way for third parties to carry the credential (certification, bureau or ATS distribution, reviews); and, for most, an exit by acquisition into a volume-hiring platform. The cases with independent evidence (Harambee's RCT, Pymetrics' audit, Saville's BPS review) are far fewer than those relying on self-published science.

### Cited Findings

**Hogan (personality at work)**

### Inferences
- Asset inventory by firm, as evidenced here: Hogan = technical manuals, validity archive, local validation reports, third-party review listings. Saville = public technical handbook, head-to-head comparative validation, BPS review. Pymetrics = one published, paid, narrowly scoped external audit. Harambee = independent RCT plus scale. HiringBranch and FurstPerson = vertical focus, named enterprise clients, trade-body and ATS distribution. - Head-to-head comparison against incumbents (Saville's Epsom approach) is the fastest route to a "we are better at X" claim, and is feasible in a niche where incumbents have no local norms.
- The common end state is acquisition by a volume-hiring platform (FurstPerson into OutMatch/Harver; Talent Q into Hay Group). Niche authority has so far been a route to being bought more often than to independent scale.

## 7. Credibility infrastructure for an assessment publisher: what it is and what it costs

### Takeaway
The infrastructure is a stack of documents and third-party checks: a technical manual built to recognised standards, an independent review or registration (BPS/EFPA model, HPCSA classification in South Africa), and, for algorithmic tools, a bias audit. Few public prices exist; the two firm data points are HPCSA classification at R10,000 per submission (older source) and Pymetrics' USD 104,465 academic audit. The BPS review route may currently be closed to new submissions.

### Cited Findings
- [INDEPENDENT] BPS test reviews are carried out independently by two reviewers and two editors against the EFPA Review Model, overseen by the BPS Testing Standards Committee; a test that passes is registered for 5 years, listed on the Psychological Testing Centre site, and the publisher gets a certificate usable in promotion. — [BPS: about test reviews and registration](https://www.bps.org.uk/about-bps-test-reviews-and-registration-0); [BPS Psychological Testing Centre](https://bps.org.uk/psychological-testing-centre)
- [INDEPENDENT] One BPS page states the test review process "is currently undergoing changes, during this time we will not be accepting tests for review" and will reopen shortly. The page date was not shown, so confirm on the live site. — [BPS: submit a test for review](https://bps.org.uk/submit-test-review)
- [INDEPENDENT] Publisher submission fees are not public; the fee schedule is in an application pack. Non-members can buy an individual review for GBP 16.48. The BPS also runs test-user qualifications and a Register of Qualifications in Test Use, and has a test-publisher directory. — [BPS: submit a test for review](https://bps.org.uk/submit-test-review); [BPS: Register of Qualifications in Test Use](https://www.bps.org.uk/join-rqtu); [BPS: find a test publisher](https://bps.org.uk/find-test-publisher)
- [INDEPENDENT] South Africa: the HPCSA Professional Board for Psychology's Psychometrics Committee *classifies* tests (decides whether a test is psychological and who may use it); one source gives the fee as R10,000 per submission with two expert reviewers, but the source is older and the fee may have changed. — [PsySSA: Psychological Testing and Assessment](https://www.psyssa.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Psychological-Testing-and-Assessment.pdf); [HPCSA regulations](https://www.hpcsa.co.za/Uploads/professional_boards/psb/regulations/regulations_gnr993_2008.pdf)
- [INDEPENDENT, legal] Section 8(d) of South Africa's Employment Equity Act required psychological tests to be "certified by the HPCSA". In 2017 the Association of Test Publishers of South Africa challenged it and the High Court held the certification requirement irrational, noting the HPCSA only classifies and does not certify. A draft amendment would remove the requirement; whether it has been enacted was not confirmed. — [Webber Wentzel update](https://www.webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/psychological-testing-and-other-similar-assessments-of-employees-an-update.aspx)
- [INDEPENDENT] NYC Local Law 144 (automated employment decision tools): an annual bias audit by an independent auditor with no involvement in building the tool and no financial interest in the employer or vendor; results posted publicly; civil penalties of USD 500–1,500 per violation per day. — [NYC DCWP FAQ](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dca/downloads/pdf/about/DCWP-AEDT-FAQ.pdf); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/practical-considerations-for-bias-audits-under-nyc-local-law-144)
- [INDEPENDENT] Compliance with that law has been weak and enforcement criticised: an academic study is titled "Null Compliance", and a law firm note of 30 January 2026 reports a critical official audit of the law signalling increased risk for employers. — [arXiv: Null Compliance](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01399v1); [DLA Piper, 30 Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers)
- [INDEPENDENT] Price point for an academic audit: USD 104,465 paid by pymetrics to Northeastern (2020). — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/02/11/1017955/auditors-testing-ai-hiring-algorithms-bias-big-questions-remain/amp/)

- [SELF-CLAIM] The documentary standard incumbents hold themselves to: Hogan organises manuals around the Uniform Guidelines and APA Standards and points to Buros and BPS reviews. — [Hogan: how to select an assessment](https://www.hoganassessments.com/thought-leadership/how-to-select-an-assessment/how-to-select-an-assessment/); [Hogan white paper](https://gmac.com/~/media/files/gmac/reflect-training/hogan-assessments-white-paper)
- [INDEPENDENT] PsySSA has published a "Call for increased psychometric practice in Africa" (content not read). — [PsySSA](https://psyssa.com/?p=31627)

### Inferences
- Cash cost of the stack appears modest next to the cost of the evidence behind it: registration and classification fees are in the low thousands of dollars; the expensive items are the validation studies themselves (data collection with employers, 12-month outcome follow-up) and any external audit (order of USD 100,000 for a university-led one, on the single data point available).
- For a Nigeria-first venture no local gatekeeper appears to exist (see section 8), so credibility has to be imported: build the technical manual to EFPA/BPS and SIOP/APA structure from day one, so that it can be submitted for BPS review when the route reopens and for HPCSA classification before selling in South Africa.
- South Africa is the one African market with a legal testing regime (the Employment Equity Act requires tests to be valid, reliable, fair and unbiased). Meeting it is both a barrier to entry there and a portable credential elsewhere on the continent.
- A voluntary bias audit in the NYC format is cheap relative to its signalling value and, given documented low compliance among vendors, still differentiates.

### Gaps
- No ISO 10667 certification pricing or list of certified providers was found; it is unclear how commonly assessment vendors certify against it.
- Buros review submission process and cost; EFPA's own review arrangements outside the BPS; SIOP Principles compliance practice: not found or not searched.
- ASSA (Assessment Standards South Africa): the search returned nothing and the retry was cut off by the budget. Current status of the Employment Equity Act amendment to section 8(d) is unconfirmed.

## 8. Professional infrastructure for industrial-organisational psychology in Nigeria

### Takeaway
The infrastructure is thin but real: there are I-O academics, at least one MSc route (University of Ibadan), a small I-O association alongside the Nigerian Psychological Association, and a recent Cambridge University Press chapter on employment-testing fairness in Nigeria. No evidence was found of a test-review body, test-user qualification or enforced testing regulation, which means a venture cannot borrow a local stamp and could become a reference point itself.

### Cited Findings
- [INDEPENDENT] A body called the Nigerian Association of Industrial and Organizational Psychologists (NAIOP) is listed on academics' profiles separately from Nigerian Psychological Association (NPA) membership; one profile lists "foundational full member" status and another "founding member of [the] National Association of Industrial and Organisational Psychologists", which suggests a recently formed body with naming still unsettled. No official website, roster or conference listing was found. — [Academia.edu profile](https://iiste.academia.edu/AdeboyeAyinde); [Nnamdi Azikiwe University staff profile](https://profile.unizik.edu.ng/nau8206)

### Inferences

## 9. A realistic 3-year roadmap from new entrant to recognised niche authority

### Takeaway
No firm studied published a roadmap, so this section is inference from what they did. The pattern supports a sequence of: document to an importable standard and name a narrow claim (year 1); produce outcome-linked local validity and fairness evidence with an independent co-author and get it reviewed (year 2); then let third parties carry the credential through certification, distribution and a recurring benchmark (year 3). The neighbouring markets warn that science alone did not save LenddoEFL and that vendor-run evidence is discounted by half.

### Cited Findings
- [INDEPENDENT] Vendor-run validity evidence is discounted: integrity test validities were .27 in publisher-authored studies versus .12 in non-publisher studies. — [Van Iddekinge et al. 2012](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-Criterion-Related-Validity-of-Integrity-Tests/9984380535302771)
- [INDEPENDENT] The highest-credibility asset in the set is an independent RCT on the organisation's own operations: Harambee, founded 2011, had its certification experiment published in the *American Economic Review* in 2022 (working paper July 2020), about nine years after founding. — [AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20200961); [Harvard CID WP 383](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/cid/publications/faculty-working-papers/job-search-and-hiring)
- [INDEPENDENT] An external audit can be commissioned and published inside a year: pymetrics' audit ran in summer 2020 and appeared at FAccT in early 2021, at a cost of USD 104,465, and drew criticism over funding, scope and pre-notification. — [FAccT paper](https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/amislove/publications/Pymetrics-FAccT.pdf); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/02/11/1017955/auditors-testing-ai-hiring-algorithms-bias-big-questions-remain/amp/)
- [INDEPENDENT] External review registration lasts 5 years under the BPS/EFPA model, with two reviewers and two editors; the route may be temporarily closed. — [BPS](https://www.bps.org.uk/about-bps-test-reviews-and-registration-0); [BPS submit a test](https://bps.org.uk/submit-test-review)
- [SELF-CLAIM] Incumbent documentary norm: a technical manual per assessment plus a technical report for every local validation study, structured on the Uniform Guidelines and APA Standards. — [Hogan](https://www.hoganassessments.com/thought-leadership/how-to-select-an-assessment/how-to-select-an-assessment/)
- [SELF-CLAIM] Publishing the technical handbook openly and running head-to-head comparisons against incumbents was Saville's route to a "more valid than X" claim within a few years of founding. — [Wave handbook](https://www.savilleassessment.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Chapter-20-Validity.pdf); [Personnel Today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/saville-consulting-questionnaires-are-superior-measures-of-job-performance)
- [SECONDARY] Vertical specialists reached named enterprise clients and trade-body presence without published science (HiringBranch: founded 2017, Fortune coverage 2023, SHRM exhibitor 2025), and the contact-centre specialist FurstPerson operated for 23 years (1997–2020) before being acquired. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/09/26/recruiters-using-hiringbranch-ai-software-elvaluate-job-candidates/); [CB Insights: FurstPerson](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/furstperson); [Mergr](https://mergr.com/transaction/harver-acquires-furstperson)
- [SECONDARY] Scientific pedigree did not guarantee survival: LenddoEFL, with Harvard origins and World Bank and IDB studies behind it, is listed as defunct with services reportedly ceasing in 2021. — [Wikipedia: Lenddo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenddo)
- [INDEPENDENT] The October 2025 CBN agent guidelines (exclusivity from 1 April 2026, board-level liability for agent due diligence) are the nearest dated regulatory trigger in an adjacent market. — [CBN guidelines](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2025/CCD/CIRCULAR%20AND%20GUIDELINES%20FOR%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20AGENT%20BANKING%20IN%20NIGERIA%20OCTOBER%206%202025.pdf); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/regulatory-update-key-legal-and-compliance-changes-in-the-cbn-guidelines-for-the-operation)

### Inferences
All of the following is the researcher's synthesis, not something any source states.

- **Year 1: make the claim narrow and the documentation importable.** Pick one role family and one outcome (for example 90-day attrition and quality scores for Nigerian contact-centre and teller hires). Write a technical manual to EFPA/BPS and SIOP/APA structure before there is much data, and version it publicly as Saville did. Appoint a named scientific advisor with I-O standing (Ibadan, OAU, or an author of the Cambridge Nigeria chapter) to borrow founder-level pedigree. Instrument every client deployment to capture outcomes, because the outcome-linked local dataset is the asset the credit-scoring cases show mattered. Publish reliability and first subgroup statistics even if small.
- **Year 2: produce evidence someone else signs.** Run at least one predictive validation with an academic co-author who holds the data analysis, on the model of the Peru study's "no vendor co-author" design, and pre-agree publication regardless of result to avoid the Pymetrics criticisms. Commission a bias audit in the NYC Local Law 144 format. Submit for HPCSA classification if South Africa is a target and for BPS review if the route is open. Run a head-to-head against whatever the client used before (interview scores, a generic aptitude test). Start a candidate-held certificate, which the Harambee RCT suggests has value in its own right.
- **Year 3: let third parties carry the credential.** Launch a short certification for client HR users and for partner consultancies; publish an annual Nigerian frontline hiring benchmark from the norm data; take a visible role in NAIOP/NPA and a contact-centre or banking industry body; integrate with the applicant-tracking and identity vendors that own the onboarding slot. Extend to one adjacent product chosen from the evidence above: training simulation for the same roles (lowest technical distance), or agent onboarding suitability for one fintech principal (regulatory tailwind, severe price anchor).
- **What three years will not deliver.** Peer-reviewed publication of a rigorous study typically lags fieldwork by years (Harambee: working paper 2020, journal 2022, on a 2011-founded organisation). A realistic three-year target is a public technical manual, one independent working paper, one published audit, and one external registration, not a body of peer-reviewed literature.
- **Commercial lesson from the neighbours.** LenddoEFL shows that being right is insufficient if the paying party does not capture the benefit; FurstPerson and Talent Q show that the usual reward for niche authority is acquisition by a platform. A founder should decide early whether the target is to be the acquired specialist or the platform, because the evidence strategy is the same but the distribution strategy is not.

### Gaps
- No source describes a deliberate "become the authority" plan from any of these firms; timelines above are reconstructed from scattered dates.
- Costs for each roadmap step in Nigeria (academic collaboration fees, data collection, psychologist salaries) were not researched.
- No African assessment publisher's path to recognition was researched (for example South African publishers such as JvR or TTS); that comparison would be closer to the venture's situation than the UK and US cases.
- Whether Nigerian enterprise buyers actually ask for technical manuals, audits or registrations in procurement was not established; if they do not, the infrastructure matters mainly for multinational clients, South African expansion and acquirers.
