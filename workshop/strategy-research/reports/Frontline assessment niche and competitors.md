# Nigerian contact centres validate, but cannot scale

The research supports the board's "Nigerian contact centres first" choice as the first validation site and contradicts it as the first market. The only sourced headcount for Nigerian BPO and contact centres is 16,540 workers (2021), against 150,000–270,000 in South Africa and 240,000–300,000+ in Egypt, and Nigerian centres screen today with a typing test and an interview, so there is no assessment budget line to capture. Agent networks hold roughly 100 times more people (1.4–2 million agents), but no source shows any principal paying for behavioural screening, the regulator defines due diligence as identity, credit and location, and the price anchor is an ID lookup at ₦10–₦60. The niche the evidence supports is job-specific behavioural simulation for Nigerian customer-facing frontline roles, proven against local outcomes and sold to in-house contact centres at fintechs, telcos and banks and to the outsourcers that employ their staff, with South African contact centres as the scale market and agent-network field staff as the second role. No player was found selling a Nigeria-built, job-specific simulation in local languages; the nearest are Dragnet, which holds a ten-year distribution deal for a US simulation library, and ShiftMate in South Africa. What is defensible is evidence and trust design, not the engine: no independently verified criterion-validity figure was found for any vendor's contact-centre simulation, and no Nigerian criterion validation of any selection test was found at all. Every figure below comes from search-result summaries, with page fetching blocked and the search budget exhausted, so this report frames the decision and the 20–25 buyer calls must make it.

## Every figure here is a search summary, not a verified page

Five researchers produced the underlying notes on 20 November 2026 under two constraints that shape how far any number can be pushed. Page fetching was blocked for every host tried, so no vendor page, technical manual, regulator text or journal article was read in full; every finding is what a search engine's summary said the page says. The shared search budget then ran out before the last batch of queries in each stream, so several planned topics were never searched. This report does not fill those holes from memory. Where the notes carried a claim without a source link, it is left out; where the researchers flagged a figure as vendor marketing, aggregator data, a single low-authority source or of uncertain page attribution, the flag is repeated here.

Three practical rules follow. Treat every number as "reported by" and re-check any figure that will carry weight with an investor or buyer against the primary page. Treat "none found" as untested, because an absence in blocked, budget-limited searching is weak evidence of absence; this applies in particular to the claims that no global vendor has a Nigerian office, African norms, African-language content or an offline mode. And treat the board's workshop figures (prices of USD 1.50–5, buyer return of about 3.3 times for contact centres against 0.8 times for field agents, 82% gross margin) as assumptions the research did not test: no Nigerian attrition rate, cost per hire or per-candidate price was found to check them against. A consolidated list of source conflicts and unresearched topics closes the analysis section.

## Nigeria's outsourced contact centres are a 16,540-worker proving ground

### Five candidate first markets, each with a different problem

The most recent sourced count of Nigeria's BPO sector is **16,540 workers and about USD 286 million of revenue**, from a 2021 Knowledge Executive survey, now five years old ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/technology/nigerias-outsourcing-sub-sector-worth-285-8m-employs-16540/)). One weak aggregator puts formal employment at 20,000–30,000 for 2025–2026 ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/nigeria-bpo-industry-statistics-2026)). Government targets sit orders of magnitude higher, at 50,000 BPO jobs through the NATEP–Lab Four agreement ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2024/03/18/fg-collaborates-with-u-s-firm-to-outsource-50000-talent-in-marketing-customer-service-admin-others)), 1 million export-linked jobs under the 2025 NATEP relaunch ([allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202506090176.html)) and 5 million under NITDA's outsourcing strategy ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2023/05/05/fg-targets-5-million-jobs-from-outsourcing-by-2027/)); no progress report against any of them was found. Planning has to use the measured base, not the targets.

### The board's choice holds as a study site and fails as a market

The board picked contact-centre and support roles first for three reasons: clean digital outcomes, real employees with a clear legal frame, and a better buyer return than field agents. The research neither confirms nor refutes those reasons, because no Nigerian attrition, cost-per-hire or outcome-data figure was found. What the research does show is that the outsourced segment is small. An illustrative check, using assumptions of mine and not sourced figures, makes the point: 16,540–30,000 workers at the 30–40% annual attrition quoted for South African and global centres implies about 5,000–12,000 replacement hires a year; assessing 20 candidates per hire at the board's assumed USD 3 yields roughly USD 0.3–0.7 million a year for the entire segment at full penetration. The board's seed-ready target of USD 150,000–300,000 in recurring revenue would need a large share of that segment.

Two further findings sharpen the concern. Nigerian centres screen with cheap proxies: candidate accounts at Outcess and iSON describe a typing test, a written or aptitude test and an oral interview, sometimes completed in two days ([Glassdoor, Outcess](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Interview/Outcess-Interview-E2055221-RVW90218467.htm), [Glassdoor, iSON](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/iSON-Group-Interview-E3931142-RVW46363478.htm)), a 2022 PalmPay posting set a 45-words-a-minute threshold measured on a public typing site ([HotNigerianJobs](https://www.hotnigerianjobs.com/print/470994/)), and Moniepoint's support postings on Greenhouse ask for prior experience and name no test ([Moniepoint on Greenhouse](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/moniepoint/jobs/5017402101)). A simulation therefore has to beat "free typing site plus interview" on cost per good hire, and it has to create a budget line, not win one. Second, entry-level BPO work is the most exposed to automation: a Mastercard Foundation study puts 40% of tasks in Africa's sector as affected by AI by 2030, with entry-level roles at 68% of the workforce ([Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/our-research/preparing-for-ai-in-the-bpo-and-ites-sector-in-africa/)). That is a demand risk for per-candidate pricing over the venture's own five-year horizon.

The verdict: run the first validity study in a Nigerian contact centre, because that is where the founder's local-context engine applies and where a study partner is reachable, but do not describe Nigerian contact centres as the niche or size the business on them. The fintech buyer the board named, which runs both a support operation and an agent network, remains the best single host.

### Agents first fails on budget, not on volume

The agent base is real and concentrated. Reports give about 2 million banking agents ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/07/cbn-bans-agents-owning-multiple-moniepoint-opay-palmpay-terminals/)) or over 1.4 million registered ([Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/0810-49380-nigeria-central-bank-is-changing-the-future-of-nigeria-s-money-agent-business-with-its-single-principal-rule)), with Moniepoint alone at more than 400,000 active agents ([Intellinews](https://www.intellinews.com/nigeria-s-cbn-bans-multi-operator-pos-model-to-streamline-agent-banking-405273)). The Central Bank's guidelines of 6 October 2025 made each agent exclusive to one principal from 1 April 2026 ([CBN guidelines](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2025/CCD/CIRCULAR%20AND%20GUIDELINES%20FOR%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20AGENT%20BANKING%20IN%20NIGERIA%20OCTOBER%206%202025.pdf)), and commentary says board members face personal penalties for due-diligence failures ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/regulatory-update-key-legal-and-compliance-changes-in-the-cbn-guidelines-for-the-operation)). That is the "why now" the board's CEO and growth seats relied on, and it stands.

The buying evidence does not. Due diligence as reported means BVN verification, credit checks and legal clearances ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/10/07/cbn-bars-debtors-blacklisted-bvns-from-operating-as-pos-agents/)), and the only firm published naira price for those checks is **₦10 for a BVN check and ₦60 for a NIN check** ([Monnify](https://monnify.com/products/verification-service)), with another vendor advertising from USD 0.06 per call ([Dojah](https://dojah.io/blog/kyc-pricing-nigeria)). No source shows any network using an integrity or behavioural test at onboarding. Much reported fraud is committed against agents, through reversal scams and social engineering, not by them ([National Daily](https://nationaldailyng.com/nigerias-pos-banking-boom-faces-rising-fraud-threats-as-agents-bear-growing-risks/amp/)). The channel itself is in flux: the agents' association predicted that about 40% of operators would exit under the new rules ([Radio Nigeria Lagos](https://radionigerialagos.gov.ng/?p=32287)), customers are shifting to direct mobile transfers ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/10/20/pos-agents-face-uncertain-future-as-nigerians-embrace-mobile-transfers/)), and yet settled POS value rose 62.54% to ₦41.74 trillion in the first half of 2026 ([New Telegraph](https://newtelegraphng.com/h126-e-payments-sustain-rapid-traction-as-pos-deals-up-62-54-to-n41-74trn/)), which points to consolidation toward fewer, larger agents. The sections of the guidelines that define qualification and due diligence were not read, so whether a suitability screen could count toward compliance is unknown.

One opening inside agent networks is better evidenced than agents themselves. A Moniepoint oversight posting describes investigating "unauthorized onboarding" and running unannounced inspections to verify field relationship managers' claims ([Moniepoint on Greenhouse](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/moniepoint/jobs/4954618101); the researcher flagged the page attribution as uncertain). Those field staff are employees, hired in volume, with conduct outcomes the principal already tracks. They are a more credible second role than agents, and they keep the regulatory story.

### South Africa is where contact-centre assessment spend is visible

South Africa added a record **26,346 net international-facing jobs in 2025** ([ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/gbs-sector-stands-firm-amid-jobs-crunch-ai-wave/O2rQGMAE1VoMd1ea)), and its industry body expects about 150,000 more by 2030 even though the 500,000 target is out of reach ([ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/bpo-sector-unlikely-to-reach-500-000-jobs-target/Olx4z7kaLBVq56km)). The researchers' arithmetic on cited ranges, which is not a published statistic, puts gross frontline hiring at roughly 70,000–130,000 a year; my illustrative calculation above, applied to that figure, gives a pool more than ten times Nigeria's outsourced segment. Vendor-quoted costs give buyers a reason to pay: a failed hire is put at R18,000–R60,000 and recruitment alone at R2,500–R4,000 per hire ([ShiftMate, Pietermaritzburg](https://shiftmate.co.za/resources/blog/why-pietermaritzburg-bpo-companies-lose-58-of-new-hires-in-90-days-and-how-trial-to-hire-fixes-the-m), [ShiftMate, turnover](https://shiftmate.co.za/resources/blog/how-sa-call-centers-are-reducing-40-staff-turnover-with-working-interviews)). Those figures are marketing from one trial-shift vendor with no methodology, and no independent South African cost-of-attrition study was found.

South Africa is not an easier first market. A local contact-centre simulation vendor already operates there, tests that measure psychological constructs must be classified by the health professions council and used by registered professionals ([PsySSA guidance](https://www.psyssa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Employment-Equity-Amendment-Act-_-What-does-it-mean-for-psychometric-test-users.pdf)), and the founder's Nigerian-context content does not transfer without new norms. It is the scale market for year two or three, which matches the board's finance view that Nigeria alone cannot carry the venture, and it should be planned from the start so that the technical manual and data model are built to pass there.

### The niche to own is a role family and an outcome, across three buyer types

The niche that survives the evidence is defined by a claim, not a sector label: **job-specific behavioural simulation for Nigerian customer-facing frontline service roles, with scores shown to predict local outcomes such as 90-day retention and quality scores.** The buyers are in-house contact centres at fintechs, telcos and banks; outsourced BPOs; and the staffing firms that employ bank and telco frontline staff. That last group matters because about 43% of bank staff were on outsourced contracts in 2021 ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/outsourced-and-underpaid-the-silent-pain-of-nigerias-contract-workers/)), which makes the outsourcer the party that bears a bad hire. Postings already ask for "English plus any other 2 local languages" ([HotNigerianJobs](https://www.hotnigerianjobs.com/print/856019/)), a requirement no tool found tests, so multilingual service handling is a natural core of the claim.

### Seven rungs from entrant to reference point

Depth in this field is a stack of documents and third-party checks, and the eight specialist firms the researchers studied built authority in a consistent order. Nigeria has no test-review body, test-user qualification or enforced testing rule that the research found, so a venture cannot borrow a local stamp and can become the reference point itself.

|---|---|---|---|

A realistic three-year depth target is rungs one to five: a public technical manual, one independent working paper, one published audit and one external registration. The visible recruiting pool is postgraduates and faculty at Ibadan and Obafemi Awolowo, plus the authors of a Cambridge University Press chapter on employment-testing bias in Nigeria ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/global-perspectives-on-the-definition-assessment-and-reduction-of-bias-and-unfairness-in-employment-testing/twenty-questions-about-employment-testing-bias-and-unfairness-in-nigeria/0FEC0B69C264E5AAAEA5CCF3F9A64A4E)), whose content was not read. Two warnings temper the ladder. Whether Nigerian buyers ask for manuals, audits or registrations in procurement was not established; if they do not, this infrastructure pays off with multinational clients, in South Africa and with acquirers. And scientific pedigree did not save the pioneer of psychometric credit scoring, which had Harvard origins and World Bank studies and is now listed as defunct ([Wikipedia: Lenddo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenddo)).

The evidence picture is the important finding. "Audited" in this market means an adverse-impact audit under New York City's Local Law 144, not validation: the pymetrics audit explicitly did not examine whether its games predict job performance ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/02/11/1017955/auditors-testing-ai-hiring-algorithms-bias-big-questions-remain/amp/)). No criterion-validity coefficient was found for the Harver, SHL, HireVue, eSkill, Mettl or Vervoe simulations, though no technical manual could be read. Pricing is thin in the same way: only self-serve platforms publish prices, no vendor price was confirmed on a vendor's own page, and no Africa or local-currency price list was found for anyone.

The structural trend is absorption into suites. Workday completed its purchase of the frontline conversational-hiring firm Paradox on 1 October 2025 ([Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2025-10-01-Workday-Completes-Acquisition-of-Paradox)), reportedly for USD 1.0 billion ([Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/workday-completes-acquisition-of-conversational-ai-firm-paradox)); Phenom bought the psychometric vendor Plum in April 2026 ([Phenom](https://www.phenom.com/press-release/phenom-acquires-plum-ai)); and Talogy partnered with an AI simulation firm in July 2026 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/talogy-partners-with-symulate-to-advance-the-future-of-talent-assessment-302824853.html)). Global publishers reach West Africa through consultancies, not product teams: Saville's partner Batian Consulting covers Nigeria and Ghana from Nairobi ([Saville](https://www.savilleassessment.com/saville-assessment-partner-with-batian/)) and Hogan's distributor Career Connections serves Africa outside South Africa from the same city ([Hogan](https://www.hoganassessments.com/news-events/updates/hogan-distributor-career-connections-announces-strategic-joint-venture-with-adili-group/)). The planned query on SHL's and Thomas International's Nigerian resellers never ran.

The best independent evidence in the whole competitive set belongs to a neighbouring format, the AI voice interviewer. A randomised trial across 70,884 applications for entry-level customer-service jobs in the Philippines found that AI-interviewed applicants received offers 9.73% of the time against 8.7% for human-interviewed ones, were 17% more likely to be employed after 30 days, and chose the AI interviewer 78% of the time when offered the choice, with humans making every final decision ([Jabarian and Henkel](https://www.rivista.ai/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/ssrn-5395709.pdf)). Voice interviewing, which HireVue and Maki now sell, is therefore a better-evidenced rival for the same budget than any simulation, and its performance on Nigerian-accented English and code-switching is an open question in both directions.

Dragnet combines what a newcomer lacks, namely test centres, procurement relationships and certifications, with regional rights to a simulation library. The open question, which the research could not answer, is whether it sells those simulations at volume for frontline roles or keeps them as a catalogue item behind its graduate-testing business; nothing found shows a Nigerian contact-centre deployment, and its pricing, mobile support and languages were not retrieved. Dragnet is both the most dangerous competitor and a plausible partner for a supervised confirmation step. Workforce Group and Proten are competitors and channels at once, because each also supplies outsourced staff. Which vendor serves which Nigerian bank comes only from prep sites that contradict each other and is not stated here as fact.

### Adjacent players will arrive from training, identity and intermediation

Contact-centre training vendors are crossing into hiring with the same role-play engines. Zenarate sells pre-hire simulation and claims 25–30% lower frontline attrition without published method ([Zenarate](https://www.zenarate.com/use-cases/pre-hire-assessments/)), and Second Nature markets "an objective, trustworthy score for every candidate" ([Second Nature](https://secondnature.ai/use-case/human-resources/)). A trade-body article notes that most AI candidate assessments are compliant but not "performance validated" ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/mena/topics-tools/news/technology/employers-fear-using-ai-candidate-assessments)), which names the line a validated entrant can hold. Identity vendors own the onboarding slot in Nigeria: Smile ID passed 200 million checks in November 2024 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/11/29/smile-id-releases-nigerias-first-ever-ekyc-report-hits-200m-identity-verification-checks/)). Psychometric credit scoring has one live player with a claimed mobile-first behavioural assessment, Begini, for which no independent validation or named Nigerian client was found ([Begini](https://begini.helpjuice.com/en_US/start-here/what-is-begini)). Work-readiness intermediaries are the strongest substitute, because the employer buys a job-ready hire and never sees assessment as a line item; ALX Nigeria says it reached nearly 100,000 young Nigerians in 2024 ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/01/21/alx-nigeria-empowers-nearly-100000-nigerian-youths-and-wins-10-awards-in-2024)).

## The industry audits bias and calls it validation; Nigeria screens on typing speed

### Industry-wide gaps

The first gap is proof. Vendor attrition claims are single-client, before-and-after figures with undefined baselines, and a 2012 meta-analysis shows why buyers who know the literature discount them: integrity-test validity was .27 in publisher-run studies and .12 in independent ones ([Van Iddekinge et al.](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-Criterion-Related-Validity-of-Integrity-Tests/9984380535302771)). Game-based assessment still lacks criterion evidence; one peer-reviewed study states that studies of predictive validity "are still lacking" ([PMC9891208](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9891208/)). Buyers feel this as a return-on-investment problem: one analyst firm reports that only 38% of talent-acquisition leaders can show a return on their technology ([Aptitude Research](https://www.aptituderesearch.com/tag/talent-acquisition/)).

The second gap is fairness honesty. The 2022 revision of the standard validity table put work samples at .33, ahead of cognitive ability at .31 ([SIOP](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance)), which helps a simulation-led product. Applicant-sample subgroup gaps for work samples are large, though, at d = .73 in a 2008 meta-analysis ([Roth et al.](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_220143196)), and situational-judgement gaps reached d = 0.66 in a sample of 37,530 applicants ([Herde, Lievens and Shalfrooshan](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6435)); both track cognitive and reading load. These are US figures on US race categories. No African subgroup estimate for work samples was found, and South African personality data failed scalar invariance across ethnocultural groups ([SAPI study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336206276_Investigating_measurement_invariance_in_the_South_African_Personality_Inventory_English_version)), which makes imported norms a real weakness.

The third gap is integrity under generative AI and proxy test-taking, covered in the analysis section. The fourth is scoring opacity: a 2025 preprint finds that even advanced language-model judges shift scores with rubric order and scale presentation ([arXiv 2506.22316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22316)), while the industry's newest products rely on model-scored conversation. The fifth is device reach: Modern Hire's data, which is vendor-sourced and non-African, says about one-third of mobile applicants abandon when an assessment is not available on their device and that cutting 15 minutes adds only one or two points of completion ([Modern Hire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/modern-hire-reveals-new-research-on-applicant-dropout-and-assessment-completion-rates-301192559.html)).

### Gaps in the Nigerian frontline niche

Frontline screening in Nigeria is fragmented, in-house and low-tech, while graduate testing is consolidated and vendor-run. That asymmetry is the opening. Four specific gaps are evidenced. Incumbent test content is exposed: past-question packs for named vendors' tests are openly sold ([JobsRegion](https://www.jobsregion.com/dragnet-past-questions/)). Remote results are not trusted: Dragnet's own blog describes an unsupervised online test followed by a harder supervised "physical verification test" ([Dragnet blog](https://dragnetnigeria-h8e8aeeab5dkg0cg.northcentralus-01.azurewebsites.net/?p=378)). Language is untested: no hiring assessment in Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo was found, although open speech models for those languages and for accented Nigerian English now exist ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/26/the-high-stakes-push-to-build-nigerias-first-open-source-llm/)). And local science is absent: the searches found Nigerian workplace-scale validations but no criterion validation of a selection test and no published norm group.

Two further gaps are structural. Agent-network vetting has no assessment vendor at all. And candidate trust is damaged by a fraud economy in which fake aptitude tests and fees extract money from jobseekers, to the point that job boards carry a standing warning never to pay for a test ([MyJobMag](https://www.myjobmag.com/jobs/ux-writer-at-moniepoint-inc-formerly-teamapt-inc)) and a reported "₦40 billion job scam crisis" prompted tougher vetting of job adverts ([Pulse](https://www.pulse.ng/story/naira40-billion-job-scam-crisis-triggers-tougher-vetting-for-employment-ads-2025042315454969951)). Any new assessment brand will be imitated by scammers within months of launch.

## Defensibility comes from local proof and trust design, not from the engine

The engine is the least defensible asset. Training vendors already point the same kind of simulation at hiring, Dragnet can license one, and suites bundle assessment for multinational clients. Three claims should also stay out of the pitch because the evidence does not support them. "AI-proof" fails because no independent study was found on whether simulations or game-based assessments resist AI assistance; that belief rests on vendor assertion and an association article. "Fairer by design" fails because work samples carry large subgroup gaps unless reading and cognitive load are designed down and the result is measured locally. And the old line that work samples are the best predictor at .54 is superseded by the 2022 figure of .33 ([Sackett et al. 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/core-reader)); the .26 the board quotes for situational-judgement tests could not be verified in this research.

Six offers are defensible because they take time, local data or local trust to copy. The first is a **local criterion-validity study that someone else signs**, with published Nigerian norms; it would exceed the public evidence of most global simulation vendors and it is the asset the credit-scoring precedents show mattered, since the Ethiopian model was trained on local repayment outcomes ([World Bank](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/422032ab83170e5b63cbf21753e6829c-0050022022/original/Evening-the-Credit-Score.pdf)). The second is **deterministic, replayable scoring**: the founder's seeded engine lets every score be rebuilt exactly, which answers the documented instability of model-based judges and fits the Nigerian data-protection right to human intervention and to contest a decision ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1650166/automated-decisions-profiling-and-data-subject-rights-in-nigeria)). The third is **content that cannot be memorised**, through seeded variants and branching scenarios, aimed at the best-evidenced weakness of incumbent tests.

The fourth is **identity assurance built for collusion**, pairing a cheap remote screen with a short verified re-test, which matches where employers are heading and what Nigerian buyers have been trained to expect. The fifth is **data-light, mobile-first, multilingual delivery with low reading load**, which addresses device abandonment, the untested language requirement and the driver of subgroup gaps at once, and which a World Bank study supports in kind, since behavioural measures of socio-emotional skills predicted employment and income better than self-reports in Nigeria, Côte d'Ivoire and Tanzania ([World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/what-skills-really-count--examining-socioemotional-skills-that-m)); Harambee's experience shows data cost governs engagement, since usage shifted toward working hours once access became data-free ([Harambee](https://www.harambee.co.za/breaking-the-barriers-enabling-youth-employment-in-south-africa-through-digital-inclusion/)). This offer carries two caveats: the board's own notes say the engine has never been tested on a low-end Android phone, and no vendor's bandwidth or offline capability could be checked. The sixth is a **candidate-held, verifiable result**. In the Harambee trial of 6,891 workseekers, certification that candidates could show employers raised employment from 31% to 36% and weekly earnings by 34%, and results reported as coarse thirds were enough ([Oxford summary](https://mbrg.bsg.ox.ac.uk/mind-and-behaviour-projects/impact-providing-information-about-job-seekers-skills-employment-south), [AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20200961)). Incumbents' results belong to the employer and die with the application. A portable result also answers the leakage problem that hurt psychometric credit scoring, where the lender who paid for screening saw other lenders pick up the borrower ([World Bank WPS 8276](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/729911513262009360/pdf/WPS8276.pdf)), though a Ugandan trial warns that disclosure can leave lower-scoring workers worse off ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/impact-disclosing-soft-skills-certificates-recruitment-labor-market-outcomes-uganda)).

One positioning lesson from the neighbouring market shapes all six. Independent credit studies found a psychometric score worked as a second screen on top of existing data and had "limited power" when asked to overrule a conventional screen ([World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/allaboutfinance/psychometrics-tool-improve-screening-and-access-credit)). The supported hiring claim is "add this to your funnel and cut early attrition", not "replace your interview".

## Seven further analyses decide whether the numbers work

### Market size and hiring volumes need a bottom-up build

Top-down market figures are unusable. Analyst estimates of the global talent-assessment market for 2026 run from USD 6.4 billion ([MarkWide Research](https://markwideresearch.com/talent-assessment-market)) to USD 27.76 billion ([Global Growth Insights](https://www.globalgrowthinsights.com/market-reports/talent-assessment-market-122353)), the Middle East and Africa share is put at 4% by one publisher and 10% by another, and no Africa-only or Nigeria-only estimate exists. The bottom-up build needs three numbers that were not found: support and contact-centre headcount at OPay, PalmPay, Moniepoint, the telcos and their outsourcers; hires per year by employer; and private-sector applicants per hire. Public-sector drives give the only applicant ratios, such as **573,680 applications for 3,927 Customs vacancies** ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/08/05/nigeria-customs-hires-just-0-7-of-573680-job-applicants/)); the researchers infer a working range of 50–150 applications per entry-level vacancy for well-known employers.

### Buyer pain is documented globally and unmeasured in Nigeria

Global benchmarks put agent attrition at 39% in 2024 ([NICE](https://www.nice.com/answers/cost-of-high-agent-turnover-in-contact-centers)), time to peak proficiency at four to six months ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/operations-blog/rewriting-the-playbook-gen-ais-impact-on-learning-in-contact-centers)) and full replacement cost at USD 10,000–20,000 in the US ([Vonage](https://www.vonage.com/resources/articles/call-center-agent-attrition/)). No Nigerian, Kenyan, Egyptian or Ghanaian attrition rate, cost per hire or training cost was found. The most defensible local method is a salary multiple, using the rule of thumb that replacement costs half to twice annual salary ([Working Solutions](https://workingsolutions.com/blog/the-real-cost-of-attrition-in-contact-centers-and-why-talent-strategy-is-now-a-revenue-decision/)). On the fraud loss line the board wants to sell against, industry-wide digital-payment fraud losses fell 51% to ₦25.85 billion in 2025 from a 2024 figure inflated by one ₦31.1 billion incident ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/nibss-digital-payment-fraud-drops-51-to-25-85b-in-2025)), and no breakdown by agent channel or by agent-perpetrated fraud exists.

### Substitutes transfer risk or bundle the whole outcome

Guarantors are standard in Nigerian hiring; one commercial guide says most employers require one or two guarantor letters ([Awajis](https://awajis.com/?p=53583)). A guarantor shifts loss after the fact and screens for social capital, so an assessment sits alongside it. The other substitutes are the typing test and interview, probation, referrals, staffing firms, and academies that assess, train and place. Nigerian staffing firms as substitutes, the use of probation as a screen and referral prevalence were never searched.

### Pricing benchmarks collide with a local anchor near zero per head

Global reference points are about USD 3.40 per candidate for generic tests and about USD 4.50 per applicant for a contact-centre conversational simulation, both from third-party sources. The Nigerian anchor is Jobberman's flat fee per listing with unlimited applicants; the researchers calculate that at 300 or more applicants this falls below ₦500 a candidate. With 50–150 applicants per vacancy, any per-candidate price multiplies accordingly, so buyers will push assessment late in the funnel or demand very low unit prices. The board's kill threshold of a median acceptable price under USD 1.50 is therefore a live risk, and the model to test is a cheap top-of-funnel tier plus a per-shortlisted-candidate or per-hire-equivalent price. In agent onboarding the comparison is an identity check at ₦10–₦60. No per-candidate price from Dragnet, Workforce Group, Proten or any global vendor's Africa list was found; this needs vendor quotes and buyer interviews.

### Regulation asks for human review now and nothing on validity

Nigeria's Data Protection Act section 37 gives candidates a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions and applies to e-recruitment ([Templars](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2025/08/Legal-Boundaries-of-AI-Driven-Recruitment-in-Nigeria.pdf)). The implementing directive took effect on 19 September 2025 ([NDPC](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf)) and, per commentary whose article numbers were not checked against the text, requires an impact assessment for profiling ([Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1700306/gaid-2025-unpacked-series-ii-data-protection-officers-mandatory-consents-and-emerging-technologies)). That commentary also says controllers relying solely on automated processing must first obtain express consent and show safeguards against bias, which is a further reason to keep a human decision-maker in every adverse outcome, as the board's red lines already require. On South Africa, the research settles the board's internal disagreement in part: the amendment that commenced on 1 January 2025 removed the requirement that tests be certified by the health professions council ([ENSafrica](https://www.ensafrica.com/news/detail/9505/commencement-of-the-employment-equity-act-ame)), but classification of psychological tests and use by registered professionals remain legal requirements ([PsySSA](https://www.psyssa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Employment-Equity-Amendment-Act-_-What-does-it-mean-for-psychometric-test-users.pdf)). A late-2026 law-firm update that still refers to a "draft proposal" could not be opened, so a newer measure may exist.

Further afield, the European Union moved its high-risk obligations for employment AI from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)), and New York's bias-audit law was found weakly enforced, with auditors identifying 17 potential violations where the city agency found one ([New York State Comptroller](https://www.osc.ny.gov/press/releases/2025/12/dinapoli-new-yorkers-deserve-transparent-hiring-process-when-artificial-intelligence-used-vet-their), [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers)). No African jurisdiction covered imposes a validity or bias-audit duty on hiring assessments, so publishing such evidence is a differentiator, and procurement questionnaires, not regulators, will drive demand for it. ISO 10667, the SIOP Principles and the International Test Commission guidelines, which the board named as its credibility bar, were not retrieved.

### AI cheating matters less here than proxies and collusion

Model capability against unproctored tests moved fast: GPT-4 scored below the 20th percentile on a number-series test in 2024 and the o1 model reached about the 95th percentile in 2025 ([Hickman 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389428748_All_your_base_are_belong_to_us_The_urgent_reality_of_unproctored_testing_in_the_age_of_LLMs)). Actual use is lower than capability implies: in a 2025 study of 3,356 candidates, more than 23% of an unwarned group admitted some generative-AI use, falling to 10.6% after a consequences warning ([Robie et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijsa.70056)), while one detection vendor flags 38.5% of AI-run interviews ([Fabric](https://fabrichq.ai/blogs/state-of-cheating-in-interviews-in-2026-tools-trends-and-prevention)). Neither number is a true rate, and no data on African candidates was found. The lesson is that any resistance claim has a short shelf life and needs recurring red-team evidence.

The Nigerian evidence points at a different threat. The national admissions board reported **4,251 cases of biometric fraud** in its 2025 exam, often with test-centre collusion ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/featured/jamb-uncovers-4251-cases-of-biometric-fraud-in-2025-utme/)), a government committee found 190 AI-assisted impersonations using image morphing ([IJRTMR](https://www.ijrtmr.com/archives/biometric-fraud-syndicated-collusion-and-the-governance-of-technology-enabled-examination-malpractice-in-nigeria-evidence-from-the-2025-utme-special-committee-investigation)), and the UK nursing regulator concluded that hundreds of candidates obtained a competency test fraudulently at a Nigerian test centre where proxies sat it ([Nursing Times](https://www.nursingtimes.net/professional-regulation/nigerian-nurses-suffering-following-nmc-fraud-allegations-05-12-2024/)). This supports the board's technical view that proxies outrank AI, and adds that a supervised centre is not a sufficient control when the centre is compromised. All of it comes from admissions and licensing exams; no proxy rate for private-sector recruitment tests was found. One caution for design: AI-text detectors reportedly misflag non-native English writers 61% of the time, a figure relayed by a vendor blog with the original study unopened ([Matchcard](https://matchcard.co.uk/blog/ai-cheating-in-hiring-assessments/)), so text detection is a poor integrity layer for this population.

### Risks, buyer questions and the holes in this research

The risks rank as follows on the evidence. The market is too small in Nigeria alone, and the scale market has a regulated testing regime and a local competitor. The local price anchor sits below the board's floor. Entry-level contact-centre work is exposed to automation, and Nigerian fintech hiring is volatile, with Kuda issuing over 100 termination letters in January 2026 ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/kuda-layoffs-2026-nigeria-fintech-restructuring/)). Dragnet can activate its simulation licence, and training vendors and suites can add a Nigerian hiring module. The benefit can leak to employers who did not pay. Subgroup differences can surface in local data. And scammers will copy the brand.

The discovery calls should settle the questions the desk research could not. How many frontline hires does each buyer make a year, from how many applicants, and what does the funnel cost today? What are 90-day attrition and time to proficiency, and what naira figure does the buyer's finance lead attach to a failed hire? Who owns the budget when no assessment line exists: operations, risk or human resources? Will the buyer share outcome data under contract? Does procurement ask for a technical manual, audit or registration? What do Dragnet and Workforce Group charge per candidate, and has anyone been offered HR Avatar simulations for frontline roles? Do outsourcers buy third-party assessments at all? At agent networks, does the principal read the 2025 due-diligence duty as covering suitability, who hires and supervises field relationship managers, and what does it pay per agent onboarded?

## Conclusion

The board framed the first decision as contact centres against agents. The evidence reframes it as a sequencing of three different things that had been bundled: where to prove the instrument (a Nigerian fintech or outsourcer contact centre), what to call the niche (customer-facing frontline service roles in Nigeria, defined by a validated claim), and where the revenue scales (in-house and outsourcer buyers first, South African contact centres next, agent-network field staff as the regulatory-tailwind extension). Under that sequencing the founder's engine matters less as a product than as an audit trail: determinism and replay are what make an independent study, a contestable score and a non-memorisable test possible, and those are the things incumbents have not shown.

The research also relocates the main commercial risk. The board worried most about validity evidence arriving late; the notes suggest price is the earlier threat, because the local anchors are a flat listing fee and a ₦10 identity lookup, and nobody was found paying per candidate for frontline behavioural screening in Nigeria. The discovery calls should therefore test willingness to pay and funnel position before they test enthusiasm for simulations, and the plan should treat a candidate-held credential and a per-shortlist price as first-class design options, not later features. Given how much of this report rests on unread pages, the two cheapest next steps are to re-run the blocked fetches on the dozen figures that carry the argument and to obtain the Knowledge Executive, BPESA and Caribou reports that probably contain the country tables this research could not open.
