## 1. Verdict: venture-scale, yes with conditions

The volume is real: Nigeria has roughly 1.5M+ point-of-sale (PoS) agents, and Moniepoint alone reports 400,000+ ([TechCabal, 2025-10-07](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/07/cbn-bans-agents-owning-multiple-moniepoint-opay-palmpay-terminals/); [Technext, older data](https://technext24.com/industry-analysis/opay-moniepoint-pos-agents-nigeria/)). Local incumbents are cheatable aptitude tests. But the venture case rests on distribution, not product. Two conditions would change my verdict:

1. **An anchor buyer with continuous volume**: at least 1,000 candidates a month, willing to share 90-day outcomes (retention, disputes, fraud). If no fintech or business process outsourcing (BPO) firm agrees within six months, this is a consultancy.
2. **A distribution partner** (staffing firm or job board) that multiplies founder-led sales. One solo founder selling one enterprise at a time is not venture scale.

Two challenges to the founder's view:
- **Outcome data rights.** Nigerian HR teams will resist sharing. Ask for it in pilots, but the early moat is more likely *workflow position*: being the default screen inside a staffing firm's process.
- **Multi-country from the start** is a pitch, not a go-to-market plan. Start with one country, one role, one buyer type.

## 2. Niche ranking (growth seat)

1. **A, frontline hiring, entered through agent networks and contact centres.** Clearest pain, highest volume, fintechs that buy fast. The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) rule making agents exclusive to one principal from 1 April 2026 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/07/cbn-bans-agents-owning-multiple-moniepoint-opay-palmpay-terminals/)) makes each network more accountable for its agents' quality [V on enforcement]. Agents are often independent merchants, not employees. So the buyer may be the head of agency banking or risk, not HR, and the pitch is "fewer disputes, less fraud and churn". That is a faster sale, but Compliance must define its legal frame.
2. **B, programme selection.** Few buyers, huge funnels. The Tony Elumelu Foundation (TEF) had about 200,000 applications for 3,000 places in 2025, with EY running selection since 2023 ([TEF 2025 cohort release](https://www.tonyelumelufoundation.org/?p=27700)). Good for logos and norm data, but seasonal, donor-funded, and EY holds the seat. Use B as a **credibility side-door**, not the core.
3. **C, MSME credit.** Biggest prize, worst start for growth: risk committees, long cycles, regulator attention, and LenddoEFL's mixed record raised in every pitch [V]. Revisit once A has validated data.

**D: no separate niche.** The UK is a later fundraising story, not a year-one market.

**Channels, in priority order:**
1. **Founder-led direct sales** to 2–3 fintech design partners.
2. **Staffing and outsourcing firms** (Workforce Group and peers) on revenue share, with outcome data rights kept by us.
3. **Job boards** (Jobberman) as a white-label or listed assessment.
4. **Applicant tracking system (ATS) marketplaces, later.** SAP now owns SmartRecruiters, whose marketplace lists assessment partners ([HR Brew, 2026-03-06](https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/2026/03/06/sap-launches-smartrecruiters-hiring-platform-integration-into-successfactors); [Testlify listing](https://marketplace.smartrecruiters.com/partners/testlify)). That suits multinationals and export BPOs, but most Nigerian frontline hiring runs on Jobberman, WhatsApp and spreadsheets [V]. A WhatsApp invite link is the first integration that matters.

**Positioning.** SHL, HireVue and TestGorilla are desktop- and graduate-oriented, with mostly non-African norms [V]. Local incumbents (Dragnet, Jobberman, in-house CBT) test reasoning, which AI and "past questions" defeat. The line: **"See how they handle a real cash dispute before they handle your customers."** Realistic, under 15 minutes, offline on a cheap phone, local languages. No "AI-proof" or "predicts performance" claims until validation shows it.

**Candidate experience as growth.** Always free to candidates: scams are common enough that Workforce Group warns candidates never to pay for a test ([MyJobMag](https://www.myjobmag.com/jobs/job-vacancies-at-workforce-group-13)). Offline, under 5 MB of data [V target], Pidgin and English first, short feedback for everyone. A respected candidate becomes a referral in a market of millions of applicants.

**Funnel and sales cycle** (estimates [V]): discovery call → agreed problem metric → free pilot (500 candidates) → 90-day outcome readout → paid annual contract → more roles and countries.
- **Fintechs:** 4–8 weeks to pilot, 4–6 months to paid. **BPOs:** similar, more price-sensitive.
- **Banks:** 6–12 months (vendor due diligence, NDPA impact assessment).
- **Channel partners:** 2–3 months to a co-pilot; they will push for exclusivity.

## 4. What carries over from Ọjà Lab, and what must be new

**Carries over:** Nigerian context and Pidgin voice craft (the scenes are the marketing); offline low-end Android delivery; deterministic replay (showing a buyer exactly what a candidate saw and did is a strong demo); the claims evidence file, as the rulebook for marketing claims.

**Must be new:** a separate, serious B2B brand (a "business game" brand undermines HR and risk buyers' trust); an employer dashboard and shortlist export; outcome-based case studies; channel partner terms; candidate support; a consent flow built around the employer–candidate power imbalance.

## 5. Risks and red lines

- **Red lines:** no prediction or "reduces fraud by X%" claims before validation; no fake logos or testimonials; never charge candidates; never sell as a tool for watching agents after onboarding.
- **Risks:** channel partners commoditise us or keep the data; Dragnet or Jobberman copies the format (defence: speed and local norms); prices far above local aptitude tests stall adoption [V].
