# WS-001 memo: Assessment science (occupational/organisational psychologist seat)

**Date:** 2026-11-20 · **Seat:** Assessment science (sub-agent, no authority) · **Status:** draft for synthesis. Sources were accessed 2026-11-20. **[V]** marks a claim I could not check against a primary source.

## 1. Verdict: venture-scale, **yes with conditions** (today the science is at "not yet")

There is no measurement asset yet. Investors and enterprise buyers pay for **evidence that scores predict outcomes fairly**, not for the engine. Two conditions would turn this into a clear yes:

1. **One local criterion study.** One role, one or more employers, at least 250 people, with a pre-registered plan. It must show an uncorrected validity of at least .20 against a business outcome (early attrition, QA score, cash shortages) and impact ratios of at least 0.80 for gender and language groups, or an explained, documented gap.
2. **A credentialed I-O psychologist owns the technical manual.** This means a co-founder or chief science officer with equity, not an advisor.

I challenge two of the founder's four pillars:
- **The outcome-data moat (pillar 3) compounds more slowly than it sounds.** Criteria are noisy: supervisor ratings have an interrater reliability of about .52 (Viswesvaran et al., 1996) **[V]**. They also differ by employer, and every new role needs its own job analysis. The moat is real, but it takes years and is employer-specific.
- **"Multi-country from the start" (pillar 4) cuts sample sizes.** Each country and language version needs its own invariance and adverse-impact evidence. Go deep first, then go wide.

## 2. Niche ranking (by how well each niche can be validated)

1. **A, frontline hiring.** It has the strongest literature, high volume, criteria that arrive in 3–6 months, and a clear decision. **For the first role, pick the one with the cleanest outcome data, not the biggest market.** Contact centres and in-house fintech support or sales staff have digital QA, handle-time and attrition data. Agent-banking field agents are often not employees, and their outcomes are noisy **[V]**.
2. **D, development and onboarding of current frontline staff (my addition).** I see it as a wedge, not a destination. The stakes are low, it fits Ọjà Lab's reflective DNA, and it builds the incumbent dataset that concurrent validation needs.
3. **C, MSME credit.** Repayment is a clean criterion, but faking and coaching incentives are extreme and the published evidence is thin and mixed (see the LenddoEFL precedent). It is a later market, once a validated platform exists.
4. **B, founder and programme selection.** The criterion (venture success) is rare, slow (3–7 years) and confounded. Cohorts are tiny (20–100). It can be sold, but it cannot be validated at venture pace.

## 3. My seat's view

**Constructs for A.** Do a proper job analysis: critical-incident interviews (40 or more incidents), SME panels of field supervisors, and an incumbent task survey. My prior list of constructs to test is:
- dependability and rule-following with cash and float;
- integrity, meaning resistance to counterproductive behaviour and fraud;
- customer orientation and emotional regulation under pressure;
- practical numeracy, such as counting, reconciling and calculating commission;
- learning and following procedures;
- persuasion and drive, for sales roles only.

Design scenarios around **job tasks**, not abstract traits. Assessment-centre research shows that simulation scores cluster by exercise more than by dimension.

**Validity evidence, stated precisely:**
- **Schmidt & Hunter (1998)**, *Psychological Bulletin* 124(2): work samples .54, general mental ability (GMA) .51, GMA plus work sample .63. These are *corrected* operational validities from mostly older US data ([PDF](https://is.muni.cz/el/econ/jaro2021/MPH_AHMR/111328406/Schmidt_1998_vyber_selection_methods.pdf)).
- **Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens (2022)**, *J. Applied Psychology* 107(11). They showed that earlier meta-analyses **over-corrected for range restriction**, especially in concurrent studies. Mean validities fall by about .10–.20. Work samples drop to about .33 and GMA to about .31 ([SIOP TIP](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance); [Sackett et al., 2023, IOP](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/S175494262300024Xa.pdf/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors.pdf)).
- In the 2022 revision, structured interviews rank top at about .42 and SJTs come in at about .26 **[V, from memory of Table 1]**.
- **McDaniel et al. (2007)**: SJT validity is about .20–.26, depending on response instructions ([PDF](https://mcdaniel97.github.io/Publications/McDaniel,%20Hartman,%20Whetzel%20&%20Grubb%20(2007)%20pp%20SJT.pdf)).
- **Fair claim for pitch decks:** "Well-built simulations typically predict job performance at about r = .25–.35 in US research. We have not yet shown this locally." Nearly all of this evidence comes from the US or Europe, so transportability to Nigeria is an **untested assumption**.

**Fairness is not automatic.** In applicant samples, work samples showed Black–White gaps of d ≈ .73, not the earlier .38 (Roth, Bobko, McFarland & Buster, 2008, *Personnel Psychology* 61). Gaps for social-skill exercises were much smaller (d ≈ .21–.27) than for cognitive and knowledge-heavy ones (d ≈ .80) ([record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_220143196)). In SJTs, subgroup gaps rise with cognitive loading (Whetzel et al., 2008) **[V]**. **Design rule:** keep the reading load low, use audio prompts in local languages, and do not let literacy become the hidden construct.

**Reliability.** Simulations have low internal consistency by design, so alpha is the wrong statistic. Use **alternate-form and test–retest** reliability, plus generalisability theory if humans or LLMs score any part. The target is at least .70 for the decision composite and at least .80 for any single cut score.

**Norms.** Build **applicant** norms, not incumbent norms, with at least 300–500 per role and country **[V, rule of thumb]**. Refresh them yearly. In selection, cut scores tied to validity data and an SME standard-setting panel such as Angoff matter more than percentiles.

**Validation design and timeline:**

| Phase | Months | Sample | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job analysis | 0–2 | 8+ SMEs, 100+ task-survey respondents | Construct and criterion plan |
| Build, then pilot (no decisions) | 2–4 | 150 | Item analysis, reliability, DIF across language versions, device completion |
| Concurrent study | 4–7 | 250–300 incumbents | First validity and adverse-impact estimates |
| Predictive study | 6–18 | 500+ hires | Operational validity against 6-month attrition and performance |

The sample sizes follow from power: to detect r = .20 with 80% power at a two-tailed alpha of .05, you need about 194 people. Range restriction and criterion noise push that up, hence 250–300.

**Adverse-impact method.** It must cover all of the following:
- selection-rate impact ratios (the four-fifths rule) together with significance tests (Fisher's exact test);
- standardised mean differences (d);
- **differential prediction**: moderated regression of slopes and intercepts, which needs about 100 or more people per subgroup **[V]**;
- item-level DIF and measurement invariance across **English, Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo** versions, adapted under the ITC translation guidelines;
- the groups **gender, ethnicity or state of origin (proxy), region, urban vs rural, age, disability and device type**.

Under the NDPA, ethnicity counts as sensitive data. Collect it voluntarily, store it apart from scores, and use it only for monitoring.

## 4. What carries over from Ọjà Lab, and what must be new

**Carries over** (these are real assets):
- **Seeded determinism** gives cheap parallel forms, which help with both item exposure and WhatsApp answer-sharing.
- **Replay and the hash-chained ledger** give audit trails and let you re-score after a key changes.
- A **written reason for every score** supports candidate feedback and ISO 10667 reporting duties.
- **"Engine owns numbers, LLM writes words"** carries over, with one condition: any LLM-varied wording in scored content must be shown to be equivalent, or frozen.
- **Offline play on low-end Android, with Pidgin voices.**

**SCN-005 cannot become a selection tool:**
- Its 8 "grass to grace" areas are a development framework, not job-analytic constructs.
- Its weights are uncalibrated.
- Its self-report half will be faked under high stakes.
- Its "blind spot" gap scores are **difference scores**, which are less reliable than either of their parts.
- It is bound by DEC-001.

**SCN-005 can become two things:**
- a reflective, developmental instrument (niche D, after a proper study);
- a template for **multi-trait multi-method construct validation**, where "you said" against "you played" becomes a convergent/discriminant check.

**Must be new:** job-analysis-derived constructs and scoring keys, a technical manual, a fairness protocol, consent flows built for the employment power imbalance, and its own governance. Ọjà Lab's rules do not transfer.

## 5. Risks and red lines

**Red lines:**
- **No live hiring decisions** on unvalidated scores. Advisory, "one input among several" use applies until the concurrent study passes.
- **No validity numbers borrowed from meta-analyses** and presented as your own.
- **No unexplained LLM scoring** of free text in high-stakes use.
- **No "bias-free" claims.**

**Risks:**
- **"Resists AI cheating" is plausible but unproven.** Screen-reading agents can play simulations. Test this with red-team trials **[V]**.
- **South Africa expansion.** EEA section 8 requires tests that are scientifically valid, reliable, fair and unbiased. The HPCSA certification clause 8(d) appears to have been deleted with effect from 1 January 2025. Confirm in the Gazette ([Acts Online](https://www.acts.co.za/employment-equity-act/8__psychological_testing_and_other_similar_assessments.php)).

**The credibility bar:**
- **Nigerian buyers** will want a technical manual, local references and adverse-impact reports.
- **Multinationals and investors** will benchmark against the **SIOP Principles** (5th ed., 2018, aligned to the 2014 *Standards*; [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/industrial-and-organizational-psychology/article/principles-for-the-validation-and-use-of-personnel-selection-procedures/730C076329F88919D5C81C6E7D304FE2)) and **ISO 10667-1/-2:2020** (client and provider duties; a revision is in progress, [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/74717.html)).
- The **EFPA Test Review Model** (v2025 draft, [EFPA](https://www.infocop.es/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/efpa_test_review_model_v2025_5.pdf)) and **BPS test registration** (5-year certificate; the BPS page says submissions are currently paused, [BPS](https://www.bps.org.uk/about-bps-test-reviews-and-registration-0)) are year-2 credentials. Use them as the template for the technical manual now.

**Co-founder: yes.** Get a Chartered Occupational Psychologist (BPS), an HPCSA-registered industrial psychologist, or a PhD-level I-O psychologist, ideally with African field experience. South Africa has the deepest bench. Start fractional if necessary, but make it an equity role before any seed round. Due diligence will ask, "Who signs the validity claims?"
