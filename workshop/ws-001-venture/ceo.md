# WS-001 memo: CEO seat

**Seat:** CEO (sub-agent, no authority) · **Date:** 2026-11-20 · Draft for the Board's synthesis. Sources accessed 2026-11-20. **[V]** = unverified.

## 1. Verdict: yes with conditions, but "not yet" today

A behavioural assessment business *can* be a venture. Option A as framed is not one yet. Two conditions would make it a plain "yes":

1. **Sell against a loss line, not an HR budget.** Nigerian frontline hiring budgets are small. The money sits in fraud losses, cash disputes, agent churn and contact-centre attrition. One buyer must confirm, in naira, that we cut a cost their CFO already tracks.
2. **One validation result on a hard outcome** (dispute rate, fraud flags or 90-day retention) from real people. Ọjà Lab has not had a single real playtest, so today we have no evidence from real users.

## 2. Niche ranking

| Rank | Niche | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **D: agent-network onboarding and conduct risk, Nigeria** (a re-cut of A) | A regulator is pushing, there is a named buyer and a measurable loss. See below. |
| 2 | **A: frontline hiring (contact centres, BPO, retail, logistics)** | A real need, but buyers are fragmented and the price per candidate is low. Best as the second wedge, once we have evidence. |
| 3 | **B: accelerator and programme selection** | The closest fit to Ọjà Lab and the fastest route to paid pilots. But the ceiling is low and budgets come from donors. Treat it as a bridge for cash and evidence, not as the venture. |
| 4 | **C: MSME behavioural credit** | The biggest prize, with mixed evidence and heavy regulation. A World Bank pilot found psychometric scores useful mainly for applicants *with no credit history* (https://blogs.worldbank.org/allaboutfinance/can-psychometrics-help-bridge-gap, undated). LenddoEFL's larger claims are self-reported. A later expansion. |

**Why D.** The CBN's October 2025 rules tie each agent to one principal (bank or fintech) from 1 April 2026. They also require banks to report on their agents monthly, and they bar blacklisted BVNs (Bank Verification Numbers) from acting as agents (CBN circular, 6 Oct 2025: https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2025/CCD/CIRCULAR%20AND%20GUIDELINES%20FOR%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20AGENT%20BANKING%20IN%20NIGERIA%20OCTOBER%206%202025.pdf; TechCabal, 7 Oct 2025: https://techcabal.com/2025/10/07/cbn-bans-agents-owning-multiple-moniepoint-opay-palmpay-terminals/). Principals now own their agents' conduct. Press estimates put agents at around 2 million, with Moniepoint alone at 400,000+ **[V]**. One principal could bring more volume than a hundred contact centres.

There are two catches:
- **Agents are independent businesses, not employees.** So this is onboarding risk, closer to C than to A.
- **The market is concentrated.** Three firms reportedly hold about 85% of it **[V]**. That means big accounts if we win, but each buyer could also build this in-house.

## 3. CEO view

**Buyer and frequency.**
- **D:** the Head of Agent Network or Head of Risk signs. They pay per onboarding plus a periodic re-check, so volume keeps coming.
- **A:** the Head of Talent Acquisition at a BPO signs, often through a staffing firm. They pay per candidate, in seasonal bursts.
- **B:** a programme manager signs once or twice a year, per cohort.
- **Candidates never pay.**

**Competition and wedge.**
- **Global incumbents** (SHL, Harver, HireVue, Criteria, TestGorilla) are built and priced for online-first developed markets. Harver bought Pymetrics in August 2022 for an undisclosed sum (https://www.finsmes.com/?p=236179). TestGorilla has raised about USD 81M, including a USD 70M Series A in June 2022 (https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/13/testgorilla-scores-70m-for-skills-tests-aiming-to-replace-the-recruitment-resume-dump).
- **Local players are thin.** Growwr reportedly raised USD 200k (https://dealroom.co/news/136235-growwr-raises-200k-to-connect-african-talent-with-global-clients/, 2025).
- **The gap is real.** The honest worry is that it exists because buyers won't pay.

Our wedge has four parts:
- it runs offline on low-end Android;
- it speaks Pidgin and local languages;
- it uses realistic behaviour tasks that AI can't easily do for the candidate;
- every score is deterministic and replayable, with a tamper-evident audit trail. A risk team or regulator values that.

**Why now.**
1. **AI cheating is breaking unproctored tests.** Google and McKinsey have brought back in-person rounds (Entrepreneur, 2025: https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/google-mckinsey-reintroduce-in-person-interviews-due-to-ai/496041). Wharton reports unproctored scores about four times higher than proctored ones (https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/opinion/cheating-in-hiring-its-a-messy-expensive-ai-world/, 2025) **[V method]**.
2. **The CBN agent rules.** To be candid: the April 2026 re-onboarding wave has passed. What remains is the ongoing duty to police agents.
3. **The EU AI Act is not a reason for Nigeria.** High-risk employment rules are now delayed to 2 December 2027 at the latest (Pinsent Masons, May 2026: https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/rules-high-risk-ai-delayed-under-eu-omnibus-deal; Orrick, July 2026: https://www.orrick.com/zh-CN/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes). That matters only for a later UK or EU push.

**Challenging the founder's four tests.**
1. *Budgets.* Right in principle, but in Nigeria the budget sits with risk and operations, not HR.
2. *Volume.* Emerging-market prices will be cents to a few dollars per assessment **[V]**. Scale therefore needs a few very large buyers, which means concentration risk.
3. *Outcome-data moat.* The weakest claim. Clients resist sharing data, outcomes are noisy, and each learning cycle takes 6–12 months. Write data rights into every contract, but don't call it a moat until it has improved prediction at least once.
4. *Multi-country from the start.* Wrong for a solo founder. Go deep in Nigeria and design so the product can move to other countries later. Multi-country belongs in the pitch, not the operating plan.

**Team gaps.**
- **An I-O psychologist,** as co-founder or as an advisor with real equity. No serious buyer or investor trusts a people-decision product without one.
- **A commercial co-founder** with Nigerian fintech or BPO enterprise relationships.

The founder builds engines and products. Sub-agent seats are not a team **[V, opinion]**.

**Fundraising story.** "The behavioural trust layer for Africa's frontline workforce." To raise a pre-seed we need three things:
- one design partner;
- one validation study showing lift on a hard outcome;
- a documented fairness and consent regime.

Exits in this category are mostly undisclosed or modest, so don't lean on comparables.

**Kill signals.**
- No paid pilot within 90 days.
- No buyer will share outcome data under contract.
- No lift over a simple baseline, such as a reference check.
- Adverse impact by gender, language or region that we cannot fix.
- The top two principals say they will build it in-house.

## 4. What carries over from Ọjà Lab, and what must be new

**Carries over:**
- the deterministic seeded engine and hash-chained ledger, which become the audit trail;
- the "LLM only writes words" rule (ADR-001), which makes scores defensible;
- offline delivery on low-end Android;
- Pidgin voices;
- governance habits: decision log, privacy screening and claims evidence file.

**Must be new:**
- job analysis, constructs, norms and validation;
- role-specific scenarios;
- consent, fairness and human-review rules, built from scratch;
- enterprise security and integrations;
- a B2B sales motion.

**SCN-005 cannot be used for scoring.** Its weights are uncalibrated and it was built for self-reflection, not for selecting people.

## 5. Risks and red lines

**Risks:**
- buyer concentration and low prices;
- incumbents cutting prices to enter Africa;
- reputational harm from unfair rejections in a high-unemployment market;
- Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) duties on automated decisions.

**Red lines:**
- no decision about a real person from uncalibrated scores;
- no live candidates before a validation plan, a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) and a human-review route are in place;
- no "integrity" or "personality" claims beyond the evidence;
- no credit use without separate licensing and legal advice;
- candidates never pay.
